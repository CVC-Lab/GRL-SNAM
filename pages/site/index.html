GRL-SNAM

Geometric reinforcement learning for navigation and mapping in unknown environments: navigation as energy minimization with symplectic (Hamiltonian) dynamics, a learned policy that sets the energy's coefficients, and radius-aware collision avoidance with IPC barriers.

Source on GitHub · Developer guide · The Ariadne ports

In your browser

These pages run GRL-SNAM's own Python in the page: CPython 3.12 and numpy compiled to WebAssembly, with libcvc's Ariadne documents for the windows (libcvc's ariglx-web). Nothing is installed and nothing leaves the page. The first visit downloads about 34 MB, which the browser then caches. A desktop browser with WebGL 2 works best.

Did it learn?

grl-snam ariadne learn on the five training runs of the learning check: each held-out curve against its training twin with its marks (best, elbow, plateau, divergence), the policy's heads with their spread, gradient norms, the checkpoint table, and grl-snam learn-report's claims, each pass or fail with its evidence. The page reads the runs' learning histories and makes the claims itself.

Navigation on libcvc

GRL-SNAM's reactive navigation also runs natively on libcvc's cvc::nav runtime, with no Python and no libtorch. These demos are in libcvc's gallery.

  • nav_city_swarm: hundreds of vehicles cross a foggy city with no global plan; tune the agents, the fog and shared or private belief live.
  • nav_city_drive: convoys drive downtown Austin to rally points on the published policy; follow a lead vehicle, or drag a rally point to re-route a convoy.
  • nav_fog_ghost: a vehicle's stale map shows a wall that is not there; it detours, then erases the wall as its sensor clears the ground.
