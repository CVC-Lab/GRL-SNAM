\begin{abstract}
GRL-SNAM navigates by learning the coefficients of a potential field and driving a vehicle with
a bicycle controller. The vehicle under that controller is now a dynamic single-track model with
Pacejka tires, load transfer and actuator lag, implemented in libcvc and twinned in GRL-SNAM's
torch code. This paper writes that model as a port-Hamiltonian system on $T^*SE(2)$
\emph{exactly}, at every tire slip and not only in the linear-tire regime. On level ground it
reduces to $\se^*$, with the pose recovered by reconstruction. The dissipation is the
tires' secant stiffness, which is positive semi-definite even when a tire slides. The port is
the pair of axle forces, with the wheels' ground speeds as its conjugate output. Steering is not a
port: it only turns the directions along which the tires push, so it does no work. Each tire's
slip is measured in its wheel's frame, so the secant form leaves no remainder, and gravity on a
grade is booked as a power line of its own. The integrator's step keeps per-step energy books whose residual is second order in the
step, and an opt-in discrete-gradient step closes them exactly. libcvc's four-wheel vehicle with suspension has the same form, with one term the single
track lacks: the moments of the tires' lateral forces on roll and pitch, a one-way coupling that is
booked on its own line. Opt-in extensions put wheel spin, with a (wheel torque, wheel speed) port,
compliant contact, with a barrier energy in $H$, and the slope across the heading, with gravity in
$H$, into the same form. We also describe the learned stochastic port-Hamiltonian plant on $SE(2)$ (\code{cvc::ph})
and its energy balance, which includes the noise's power. We also describe an opt-in form of that
plant in which steering is a modulation, so that only the drive supplies power, as in the analytic
plant. We give the navigator's per-step energy
identity, with its context and projection work at each tick. Every claim is tied to a test in
libcvc or GRL-SNAM, with the tolerance the test enforces and the value measured on the released
code (Section~\ref{sec:validation}), so it can be checked independently.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

GRL-SNAM~\cite{grlsnam2025} treats navigation as motion in a learned energy landscape. A small
network predicts the coefficients $(\alpha_j,\beta,\gamma)$ of a potential: barrier weights per
obstacle, a goal spring and a damping. A controller turns the potential's force into steering and
acceleration commands for a vehicle. GRL-SNAM's own guide to the C++ port
(\code{docs/cvc-nav-and-grl-snam-guide.md}, ``A note on the vocabulary'') is careful about what
this does and does not buy. The deployed navigator is a learned-coefficient potential field with
viscous damping, on top of a kinematic bicycle and a pure-pursuit controller. No Hamiltonian is
carried through it, and no energy or passivity argument transfers to it.

This paper is about the layer under the navigator: the vehicle. Since libcvc 3.7.0, a dynamic
single-track vehicle (the plant) can replace the kinematic update where a policy asks for it.
libcvc 3.7.1 writes that vehicle as a port-Hamiltonian (pH) system with a genuine Hamiltonian $H$,
momenta, a positive semi-definite dissipation, and a power port. It keeps books of where the
vehicle's kinetic energy goes, step by step. The same release adds two related pieces: a learned
stochastic pH model of a vehicle, and per-step energy books for the navigator's point-mass
surrogate. Across the navigator's ticks those books include the potential's changes of context.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item An exact pH form of the dynamic single-track vehicle, valid at every slip
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:ph}). The dissipation is built from the tires' secants, the port
        is the axle forces, and steering is shown not to be a power port
        (Section~\ref{sec:ph}). The same form, with its books, for the four-wheel vehicle with
        suspension, and an opt-in grade force that puts the terrain's energy in $H$
        (Section~\ref{sec:four-wheel}). Opt-in wheel spin, compliant contact and the slope across
        the heading, in the same form and with their books (Section~\ref{sec:ext}).
  \item The discrete step's energy books, with a residual that is second order per step
        (Section~\ref{sec:books}), an opt-in discrete-gradient step whose books close exactly
        (Section~\ref{sec:dg}), and a live plant that keeps them for every vehicle in a
        navigation world.
  \item The learned plant \code{cvc::ph}: its structure, its energy balance including the noise
        power, its integrator and its training objective (Section~\ref{sec:learned}). It also has an
        opt-in form in which steering modulates the model instead of supplying power, as it does in
        the analytic plant (Section~\ref{sec:learned-steer}).
  \item The navigator's per-step energy identity, the two checks built on it, and the context
        work at tick boundaries (Section~\ref{sec:nav}).
  \item GRL-SNAM's side: the torch twins and their parity tiers, what is not twinned yet, and how
        a GRL-SNAM rollout or trainer can use the plant and its books (Section~\ref{sec:grl}).
  \item A validation table that maps each claim to a test, its tolerance, the measured value and
        the commit that added it (Section~\ref{sec:validation}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is not claimed.} The closed loop of navigator, controller and plant is not
port-Hamiltonian. The controller is a path follower, not an energy-shaping controller
(Section~\ref{sec:limits}). Neither the analytic plant's default integrator nor the learned
plant's is structure-preserving. Both keep books with a residual instead; an opt-in step closes the
analytic plant's exactly (Section~\ref{sec:dg}). The learned plant's filter-based
evidence bound is not yet implemented. The four-wheel vehicle's pH form
(Section~\ref{sec:four-wheel}) is passive with respect to its wheels' forces except for a one-way
lever term, and only its wheel-spin form has a GRL-SNAM twin. Wheel spin, compliant contact and
the side slope are opt-in (Section~\ref{sec:ext}): the plants' defaults, and every result outside
that section and its tables, are without them. The four-wheel plant has no compliant contact.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}

\subsection{Port-Hamiltonian systems}
An input-state-output pH system~\cite{vanderschaft2014pht} with state $x$, stored energy $H(x)$,
input $u$ and output $y$ is
\begin{equation}
  \dot x=\big(\J(x)-\R(x)\big)\nabla H(x)+\G(x)\,u,\qquad y=\G(x)\T\nabla H(x),
  \label{eq:ph}
\end{equation}
with $\J=-\J\T$ (the interconnection) and $\R=\R\T\succeq0$ (the dissipation). Along solutions,
$\dot H=-\nabla H\T\R\nabla H+y\T u\le y\T u$, so the system is passive from the port $(u,y)$, with
$H$ as storage. The structure tells you where energy goes: $\J$ moves it without loss, $\R$
dissipates it, and the port $y\T u$ is the only way it enters from outside. If $\R$ may depend on
the input, nothing in this changes, provided it is positive semi-definite for every input value.

\subsection{Planar rigid bodies and the Lie--Poisson structure}
A planar rigid body has pose $q=(x,y,\psi)\in SE(2)$ and body twist $v=(v_x,v_y,r)\in\se$: the
velocity of its centre of gravity in the body frame, and its yaw rate. With mass matrix
$M=\operatorname{diag}(m,m,I_z)$ the momenta are $p=Mv\in\se^*$, and the kinetic energy is
$H(p)=\tfrac12p\T M^{-1}p$, so $\nabla_pH=v$. The body-frame equations of motion of a free body are
the Euler--Poincar\'e equations $\dot p=\operatorname{ad}^*_vp+f$, with $f$ the applied body
wrench and
\begin{equation}
  \operatorname{ad}^*_vp=\J(p)\,v,\qquad
  \J(p)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&p_y\\0&0&-p_x\\-p_y&p_x&0\end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:J}
\end{equation}
$\J(p)$ is skew-symmetric and linear in $p$: it is the Lie--Poisson structure of
$\se^*$~\cite{marsden1999}, so it satisfies the Jacobi identity. With $M$ diagonal and equal
masses on $v_x$ and $v_y$, $\J(p)v=(mv_yr,\,-mv_xr,\,0)$. These are the familiar $+rv_y$ and $-rv_x$
terms of a vehicle's body-frame equations.

\subsection{Where the plant sits in GRL-SNAM}
GRL-SNAM's navigator evaluates a potential $U_{\mathrm{nav}}$ at the agent's position $o$,
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{nav}}(o)=\tfrac{\beta}{2}\lVert o-g\rVert^2+\textstyle\sum_j\alpha_j\,b(d_j(o))
  +\text{(material and terrain terms)},
  \label{eq:unav}
\end{equation}
with $b$ the IPC barrier of each obstacle's clearance $d_j$, and $g$ the current goal or carrot.
Damping $-\gamma v$ is dissipation, not part of $U$. An external force, where one is used, is a
port, not part of $U$ either. libcvc evaluates $U_{\mathrm{nav}}$ along every rollout
(\code{inc/cvc/nav/detail/nav\_potential.h}), and GRL-SNAM's torch twin
(\code{grl\_snam/nav\_potential.py}, CVC-Lab/GRL-SNAM\#133) is held to it. The controller
(\code{bike\_control} in \code{diff\_rollout.h}; \code{sdf\_nav.bike\_control} in GRL-SNAM)
turns the force $-\nabla U_{\mathrm{nav}}$, plus any external force, into a steering command
$\delta_{\mathrm{cmd}}$ and an acceleration command $a_{\mathrm{cmd}}$. A plant then moves the
vehicle. The default plant is the kinematic bicycle, $\dot\psi=(v/L)\tan\delta$. The dynamic plant
of this paper takes the same commands, so a policy can train through tires, load transfer and
actuator lag with the controller and its adjoint unchanged.

\section{The dynamic single-track vehicle}
\label{sec:model}

The plant is \code{cvc::nav::diff::dyn\_plant} (\code{inc/cvc/nav/detail/dyn\_bike.h}). It is
written once over a scalar type: float in training, double in the tests, and a forward-mode dual
number for its adjoint. Its torch twin is \code{grl\_snam.dyn\_bike.dyn\_plant}. The model is the
standard single-track (``bicycle'') vehicle~\cite{rajamani2012} with Pacejka lateral
forces~\cite{pacejka2012}. We summarize what the pH form needs.

\paragraph{State and parameters.} The state is $(x,y,\psi,v_x,v_y,r,\delta,a)$: the rear axle's
position, the heading, the body twist at the centre of gravity, the road-wheel steering angle, and
the drive or brake force per unit mass. The last two sit behind first-order actuators. The
parameters are the mass $m$, the yaw inertia $I_z$, the distances $l_f,l_r$ from the centre of
gravity to the axles ($L=l_f+l_r$), the centre of gravity's height $h_{\mathrm{cg}}$, the ground's
friction $\mu$, Pacejka factors $B_f,B_r$ and $C$, drag $c_d$, rolling resistance $c_{rr}$, the
drive and brake split between the axles, and a speed floor $\varepsilon>0$.

\paragraph{Loads and grip.} Quasi-static longitudinal load transfer gives axle loads
$F_{z,f}=m(gl_r-ah_{\mathrm{cg}})/L$ and $F_{z,r}=m(gl_f+ah_{\mathrm{cg}})/L$, each floored at a
small fraction of $mg$. The drive or brake force $ma$ splits between the axles, and each share is
saturated smoothly at $\mu F_{z,w}$. These are the axle forces $F_{x,f},F_{x,r}$. What grip they
leave for cornering, $P_w$, follows from the friction ellipse with the longitudinal force first.
Both depend on the actuator state $a$.

\paragraph{Directions.} Each tire's lateral force reaches the twist's coordinates
$(v_x,v_y,r)$ along $n_w$, and each axle's drive or brake force along $t_w$:
\begin{equation}
  n_f(\delta)=\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\delta\\\cos\delta\\l_f\cos\delta\end{pmatrix},\quad
  n_r=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\-l_r\end{pmatrix},\quad
  t_f(\delta)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\delta\\\sin\delta\\l_f\sin\delta\end{pmatrix},\quad
  t_r=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}.
  \label{eq:dirs}
\end{equation}
So $n_w\T v$ is the sideways velocity of that axle's contact patch in its wheel's frame, and
$t_w\T v$ is its ground speed along the wheel.

\paragraph{Slips.} Each slip is measured in its wheel's frame, as in the four-wheel model. With
$w_w=n_w\T v$ the axle's sideways velocity there, $w_f=\cos\delta\,(v_y+l_fr)-\sin\delta\,v_x$ and
$w_r=v_y-l_rr$, and $u_w=\sqrt{(t_w\T v)^2+\varepsilon^2}$ its floored forward speed, the slip
angles are
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_w=-\operatorname{atan2}(w_w,u_w),\qquad w\in\{f,r\}.
  \label{eq:slips}
\end{equation}
The floor keeps them smooth through zero speed. At the rear, $u_r=\ub=\sqrt{v_x^2+\varepsilon^2}$,
the floored speed that also smooths drag and rolling resistance. The lateral forces are Pacejka's,
$F_{y,w}=P_w\sin\!\big(C\arctan(B_w\alpha_w)\big)$, with the shape factor $C\in(1,2)$, as the
parameter's documentation requires. Before libcvc \#852 the front slip was
$\varsigma\delta-\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_fr,\ub)$, with $\varsigma=v_x/\ub$, which left the
remainder of Section~\ref{sec:floor}.

\paragraph{Equations of motion.} With $\theta$ the sine of the slope along the heading (positive
uphill), and $c_x(\ub)=c_d\ub+c_{rr}mg/\ub$ the drag and rolling resistance acting on $v_x$, the
body-frame Newton--Euler equations are
\begin{align}
  m(\dot v_x-rv_y)&=F_{x,f}\cos\delta-F_{y,f}\sin\delta+F_{x,r}-c_x v_x-mg\theta,\notag\\
  m(\dot v_y+rv_x)&=F_{x,f}\sin\delta+F_{y,f}\cos\delta+F_{y,r},\label{eq:ne}\\
  I_z\dot r&=l_f\big(F_{x,f}\sin\delta+F_{y,f}\cos\delta\big)-l_rF_{y,r}.\notag
\end{align}
In momenta and the directions above, \eqref{eq:ne} reads
\begin{equation}
  \dot p=\J(p)v+t_fF_{x,f}+t_rF_{x,r}+n_fF_{y,f}+n_rF_{y,r}-c_xv_x\,e_x-mg\theta\,e_x .
  \label{eq:ne-p}
\end{equation}
Left out on purpose: suspension, lateral load transfer and contact loss; the four-wheel model
(Section~\ref{sec:four-wheel}) adds some of these. Wheel spin and the slope across the heading are
opt-in (Section~\ref{sec:ext}).

\section{The port-Hamiltonian form}
\label{sec:ph}

\subsection{The tires' secant stiffness}
The key to an exact form is to write each lateral force as a stiffness times a velocity, in a way
that stays valid past the tire's peak. A tangent stiffness does not. The secant through the force's
root does.

\begin{lemma}[Secant stiffness]
\label{lem:secant}
With the slips~\eqref{eq:slips}, $B_w>0$, $C\in(0,2)$ and $P_w\ge0$, the secant stiffness
\begin{equation}
  S_w=-\frac{F_{y,w}}{w_w}\qquad(w_w\neq0),
  \label{eq:secant}
\end{equation}
continued at the root by the slope there, $S_w=P_wCB_wu_w/(u_w^2+w_w^2)$, is non-negative at every
slip, every speed and every steering angle, sliding included. So $F_{y,w}=-S_w\,w_w=-S_w\,n_w\T v$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $u_w\ge\varepsilon>0$, $\alpha_w=-\operatorname{atan2}(w_w,u_w)$ has the sign of $-w_w$ and
vanishes exactly at $w_w=0$: each slip's root is where its contact patch has no sideways velocity
in its wheel's frame. Since $C\arctan(B_w\alpha_w)\in(-C\pi/2,\,C\pi/2)\subset(-\pi,\pi)$, the
force $P_w\sin(C\arctan(B_w\alpha_w))$ has the sign of $\alpha_w$, or is zero. Hence $F_{y,w}$ and
$w_w$ have opposite signs, and $S_w\ge0$. At the root, $-\partial F_{y,w}/\partial w_w$ (at fixed
$u_w$) is $P_wCB_wu_w/(u_w^2+w_w^2)\ge0$.
\end{proof}

The implementation is \code{dyn\_secant\_w}. It takes the root branch when
$(B_w\alpha_w)^2<10^{-8}$, so there $F_{y,w}=-S_ww_w$ holds to second order in the slip, not
exactly. That rounding is the only remainder; it is booked on its own line
(Section~\ref{sec:floor}). A Pacejka force with $C<2$ never changes sign against its slip. A tire
past its peak gives less force for more slip, but never pushes the slip further.

\subsection{The structure}
Define
\begin{align}
  \R(v;\delta,a)&=c_x(\ub)\,e_xe_x\T+S_f\,n_fn_f\T+S_r\,n_rn_r\T,
  \label{eq:R}\\
  \G(\delta)&=\big[\,t_f\;\;t_r\,\big],\qquad u=(F_{x,f},\,F_{x,r}),\qquad y=\G(\delta)\T v,
  \label{eq:G}\\
  f_\varepsilon&=n_f\big(F_{y,f}+S_f\,n_f\T v\big)+n_r\big(F_{y,r}+S_r\,n_r\T v\big).
  \label{eq:feps}
\end{align}
$\R$ depends on the actuator state $a$ through the grips $P_w$ in $S_w$. $f_\varepsilon$ is what the
secant dissipation leaves of the tires' actual forces. By Lemma~\ref{lem:secant} it is zero off
the secants' root branch, and that branch's rounding on it (Section~\ref{sec:floor}). In code
these are \code{dyn\_ph\_J},
\code{dyn\_ph\_R}, \code{dyn\_ph\_G}, \code{dyn\_ph\_input} and \code{dyn\_ph\_floor}. Their sum is
\code{dyn\_ph\_rhs}, and \code{dyn\_ph\_rates\_at} evaluates the power balance at a state.

\begin{proposition}[The dynamic single-track vehicle is port-Hamiltonian at every slip]
\label{prop:ph}
Under the conditions of Lemma~\ref{lem:secant}, with $c_d,c_{rr}\ge0$, the vehicle's equations
of motion~\eqref{eq:ne}, with its pose's kinematics, are exactly the pH system on
$SE(2)\times\se^*$
\begin{equation}
  \begin{pmatrix}\dot q\\\dot p\end{pmatrix}=\left[\begin{pmatrix}0&T(q)\\-T(q)\T&\J(p)\end{pmatrix}
  -\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\0&\R(v;\delta,a)\end{pmatrix}\right]
  \begin{pmatrix}\nabla_qH\\\nabla_pH\end{pmatrix}
  +\begin{pmatrix}0\\\G(\delta)\,u+f_\varepsilon+f_g\end{pmatrix},\qquad y=\G(\delta)\T v,
  \label{eq:plant-full}
\end{equation}
with $q=(x,y,\psi)$ the pose, $T(q)$ its velocity map (below), $H=\tfrac12p\T M^{-1}p$, and
$f_g=-mg\theta\,e_x$ the grade's force. The structure matrix is skew and satisfies the Jacobi
identity, and $\R=\R\T\succeq0$ at every state, slip and input. Since $H$ does not depend on $q$,
$\nabla_qH=0$: the first row is the reconstruction $\dot q=T(q)v$, and the second is
\begin{equation}
  \dot p=\big(\J(p)-\R(v;\delta,a)\big)v+\G(\delta)\,u+f_\varepsilon-mg\theta\,e_x .
  \label{eq:plant-ph}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
  \dot H=-v\T\R v+y\T u+v\T f_\varepsilon-mg\theta\,v_x ,
  \label{eq:balance}
\end{equation}
and the vehicle is passive with respect to the supply $y\T u+v\T f_\varepsilon-mg\theta v_x$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first row is the pose's kinematics, since $\nabla_pH=v$ and $\nabla_qH=0$. For the second,
by~\eqref{eq:feps}, $n_fF_{y,f}+n_rF_{y,r}=-\big(S_fn_fn_f\T+S_rn_rn_r\T\big)v+f_\varepsilon$.
Substituting this into~\eqref{eq:ne-p}, with $t_fF_{x,f}+t_rF_{x,r}=\G u$ and $c_xv_x\,e_x=c_x\,e_xe_x\T v$,
gives~\eqref{eq:plant-ph}. The structure matrix is the canonical structure of $T^*SE(2)$ (below),
so it is skew and satisfies the Jacobi identity; its lower block $\J(p)$ is the Lie--Poisson
tensor~\eqref{eq:J}. $\R$ is a combination of outer products with coefficients $c_x\ge0$,
$S_f\ge0$ and $S_r\ge0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:secant}), so it is symmetric positive semi-definite. Finally
$\dot H=\nabla_qH\T\dot q+v\T\dot p=v\T\dot p$, $v\T\J v=0$ and $v\T\G u=y\T u$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The pose and $T(q)$.} $SE(2)\times\se^*$ is the cotangent bundle $T^*SE(2)$, trivialized
by left translation, and the block matrix in~\eqref{eq:plant-full} is its canonical
structure~\cite{marsden1999}. The off-diagonal blocks pair the pose with its momenta, and the
lower block is the Lie--Poisson structure of $\se^*$. For the pose of the centre of gravity,
$T(q)=R(\psi)\oplus1$, so $\dot q=T(q)v$ is $(\dot x,\dot y)=R(\psi)(v_x,v_y)$, $\dot\psi=r$. The
plant's pose is the rear axle's instead (\code{dyn\_bike.h}), as \code{bike\_step}'s is. That pose
is the centre of gravity's moved $l_r$ back along the body's axis, a fixed right translation on
$SE(2)$, so its block is
\begin{equation*}
  T(q)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\psi&-\sin\psi&l_r\sin\psi\\\sin\psi&\cos\psi&-l_r\cos\psi\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation*}
which gives $\dot x=\cos\psi\,v_x-\sin\psi\,(v_y-l_rr)$, $\dot y=\sin\psi\,v_x+\cos\psi\,(v_y-l_rr)$
and $\dot\psi=r$: the rear axle's velocity, as the step moves it (Section~\ref{sec:books}). The
two are one system. The change of pose is a diffeomorphism of the phase space that leaves $p$
alone, and it carries the canonical structure to the one with the rear axle's $T(q)$, which is
therefore skew and satisfies the Jacobi identity too. We use the rear axle's, since it is the
plant's state. The pose's kinematics is checked through \code{DynBike.Golden}, whose outputs
include the pose, in the kinematic limit through \code{DynBike.KinematicLimitFollowsBikeStep}
(Table~\ref{tab:val-plant}), and, since libcvc \#987, on its own:
\code{DynPHFull.ThePoseUpdateIsTv} finds the step's pose update to be $q_1=q_0+h\,T(q_1)v_1$ to
round-off (Table~\ref{tab:val-full}).

\begin{corollary}[Level ground: the reduced system]
\label{cor:level}
On level ground ($\theta\equiv0$), $H$ and every force in~\eqref{eq:plant-ph} are independent of
$q$, so the momentum equation closes on $\se^*$. It is then a pH system on $\se^*$ alone, with the
Lie--Poisson structure $\J(p)$, and the pose follows from its solution by the reconstruction
$\dot q=T(q)v$. Since $f_\varepsilon$ is zero but for the secants' rounding at the root, the
vehicle is then passive from the drive port $(u,y)$ alone, at every speed and every floor
$\varepsilon$.
\end{corollary}

With a grade, $\theta$ is read from the world at the vehicle's position, so the momentum equation
is coupled to the pose through that input (Section~\ref{sec:grade}). With the side slope's option,
gravity is in $H$ instead (Section~\ref{sec:full}).

\subsection{Steering is not a power port}
\label{sec:steer}
In~\eqref{eq:plant-ph} the steering angle appears only inside $n_f$ and $t_f$, and through them in
the front slip and its secant. It turns the directions along which the front tire pushes, so it
modulates the structure. It is not an effort with a conjugate flow, and it does no work on the body. The power
that drives the body is $y\T u=F_{x,f}\,t_f\T v+F_{x,r}\,t_r\T v$: each axle's force times its
wheel's ground speed. With no axle force, nothing is supplied, however the wheel turns.

This matters because the natural linear-tire pH form gets it wrong, and libcvc's own first pH form
(transfix/libcvc\#748, replaced in \#831) did. With linear tires $F_{y,f}\approx C_f\alpha_f$ and
small angles, it is tempting to take $u=(F_x,\delta)$, with input column $C_f(0,1,l_f)\T$ for
$\delta$. The conjugate output of $\delta$ is then $C_f(v_y+l_fr)$, and the supplied power
contains $C_f\delta(v_y+l_fr)$. This has no sign, so that model can gain kinetic energy from
steering alone, with no drive force. It is the first-order truncation in $\delta$
of~\eqref{eq:R}. Expanding $n_f\approx(-\delta,1,l_f)$ and, at small slip, $S_f\approx C_f/u_f\approx
C_f/\ub$, the $v_x$-column of $\tfrac{C_f}{\ub}n_fn_f\T$ contributes $+C_f\delta\,(v_x/\ub)\,(0,1,l_f)\T$
to $-\R v$, which is $C_f\delta\,(0,1,l_f)\T$ when $v_x\approx\ub$. The truncation moves that term
out of $\R$ and calls it an input, and it drops the cornering drag $-F_{y,f}\sin\delta$ on $v_x$. In
the modulated form, the front tire's power $F_{y,f}\,n_f\T v$ is the dissipation
$-S_f(n_f\T v)^2\le0$, exactly but for the root branch's rounding. Steering only redirects
energy.

\subsection{The speed floor}
\label{sec:floor}
The floor $\varepsilon$ enters the slips~\eqref{eq:slips} only through their forward speeds $u_w$,
which keep them smooth through zero speed. It does not move their roots: each slip vanishes where
$n_w\T v=0$, which is where the secant form puts it. So off the secants' root branch
$f_\varepsilon=0$ exactly, and on it $f_\varepsilon$ is that branch's rounding. The floor line of
the books is kept, and measures it. The test \code{DynBikePH.TheFloorTermIsRoundOff} finds its
worst share of the flow at most $2.2\times10^{-16}$ at every floor from $\varepsilon=0.5$ down to
$10^{-9}$~m/s.

This holds since libcvc \#852, which moved the front slip into its wheel's frame. Before it, the
front slip $\varsigma\delta-\operatorname{atan2}(v_y+l_fr,\ub)$, with $\varsigma=v_x/\ub$, had its
root at $v_y+l_fr=\ub\tan(\varsigma\delta)$, not where $n_f\T v=0$ ($v_y+l_fr=v_x\tan\delta$). The
remainder was $f_\varepsilon=S_f'\big(\ub\tan(\varsigma\delta)-v_x\tan\delta\big)\,n_f$, with $S_f'$ that
slip's secant in $v_y+l_fr$, and $\R$'s front term was $(S_f'/\cos\delta)\,n_fn_f\T$, positive
semi-definite only for $\lvert\delta\rvert<\pi/2$. The bracket is $O(\varepsilon^2/\lvert v_x\rvert)$,
and the floor's worst share of the flow fell as $\varepsilon^2$: $5.4\times10^{-4}$,
$5.4\times10^{-6}$ and $5.4\times10^{-8}$ at $\varepsilon=0.5,\,0.05,\,0.005$~m/s. Outside a crawl
the change moves the plant little: the vector field, the books' order and the kinematic limit of
Table~\ref{tab:val-plant} measure the same before and after it.

\subsection{Grade}
\label{sec:grade}
On a slope, gravity along the heading does work $-mg\theta\,v_x$. In the plant the grade is data:
\code{dyn\_plant} takes $\theta$, the sine of the slope along the heading (positive uphill), sampled
from the world each step, and does not differentiate it. So in~\eqref{eq:plant-full} it is an
outside, time-varying input $\theta(t)$, with the force $f_g=-mg\theta\,e_x$ and its own power line
$-mg\theta\,v_x$. It is not the gradient of a potential, because by default the plant has no side
slope. In
the centre of gravity's pose, gravity on a terrain $z(x,y)$ has the in-plane force
$-T(q)\T\nabla_q(mgz)=-mg\big(R(\psi)\T\nabla z,\,0\big)$. That force has a component along the
heading and one across it, and the plant keeps only the first. So $-mg\theta\,v_x=-\tfrac
d{dt}(mgz)$ only where $v_y$ or the side slope is zero (and to $O(\text{slope}^3)$, below).

libcvc \#987 adds the side slope as an option. With it, gravity moves into $H$ as
$V(q)=mg\,z(q)$, and the grade line becomes $-\Delta V$ (Section~\ref{sec:full}). That holds for
the potential in projected coordinates, whose force along the heading is $mg$ times the slope's
tangent. The plant's $\theta$ is its sine, and the two agree to $O(\text{slope}^3)$; the side
slope's option uses the tangents. The four-wheel plant already
puts its terrain in $H$, by option, through its suspension (Section~\ref{sec:four-wheel}).

\subsection{Tangent versus secant}
\label{sec:tangent}
Past a tire's peak, $\lvert B_w\alpha_w\rvert>\tan(\pi/(2C))$, its force falls as its slip grows. The
\emph{tangent} dissipation, minus the derivative of the tires' lateral force $Q_y$ and yaw moment
$Q_r$ in $(v_y,r)$, is
\begin{equation}
  D_t=-\frac{\partial(Q_y,Q_r)}{\partial(v_y,r)}=k_f\,aa\T+k_r\,bb\T,\qquad a=(1,l_f),\;b=(1,-l_r),
  \label{eq:tangent}
\end{equation}
with $k_w$ each tire's $\partial F_{y,w}/\partial\alpha_w$ times its slip's sensitivity to $(v_y,r)$
along $a$ or $b$. At the rear that is $u_r/(u_r^2+w_r^2)$. At the front, the sideways velocity $w_f$
and the forward speed $t_f\T v$ (floored to $u_f$) both move along $a$, as $\cos\delta$ and
$\sin\delta$, and the force reaches the body through $\cos\delta$, so
\begin{equation}
  k_f=\frac{\partial F_{y,f}}{\partial\alpha_f}\,
      \frac{\cos\delta\,\big(\cos\delta\,u_f-\sin\delta\,w_f\,t_f\T v/u_f\big)}{u_f^2+w_f^2}.
  \label{eq:kf}
\end{equation}
In code this is \code{dyn\_ph\_tangent\_D}; \code{DynBikePH.TheTangentIsTheTiresDerivative}
checks it against central differences of the tires' lateral force and moment, to
$1.4\times10^{-7}$ of its scale. Since $a$ and $b$ are independent, $D_t\succeq0$ exactly when both
$k_w\ge0$: when both tires are below their peak (and the front's slip angle is under about
$90^\circ-\lvert\delta\rvert$, where its sensitivity changes sign). Past the peak, the linearized
motion can be locally unstable, which is sliding. That is a statement about
stability, not about energy. The secant~\eqref{eq:R}, the dissipation in the energy
balance~\eqref{eq:balance}, stays positive semi-definite. So a positive semi-definite dissipation of
the form $-D(v)v$ can represent sliding: its gain falls with slip. What it cannot represent is a
tire that supplies energy, and a Pacejka tire with $C<2$ never does.

On the tests' full-size utility-vehicle preset (\code{DynBikePH.TheSecantStaysPSDWhereTheTangentDoesNot}),
with steer ramps to the lock and swerves at 8--20~m/s:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The vehicle understeers: the front tire passes its peak first.
  \item The tangent is positive semi-definite in every sample below about half the grip,
        $0.5\mu g$. Above it, it is indefinite in up to half the samples of a lateral-$g$ bin.
  \item The rear tire never passes its peak.
  \item The secant is positive semi-definite in every sample, at $\mu=0.9$ and at $\mu=0.6$.
\end{itemize}
This bears on any learned model that constrains $D(v)\succ0$, such as the one in
Section~\ref{sec:learned}. The constraint does not rule out sliding.

\subsection{The kinematic limit and the Jacobi identity}
The kinematic bicycle imposes a no-slip constraint at each axle. That constraint is not integrable,
so the reduced bracket is only almost-Poisson and the Jacobi identity
fails~\cite{vanderschaft1994nonholonomic}. The dynamic vehicle replaces the constraint by tire
forces, its $\J$ is a genuine Lie--Poisson structure, and the kinematic bicycle is its stiff limit:
$S_w\to\infty$ along $n_f$ and $n_r$ drives $n_w\T v$ to zero. The test
\code{DynBike.KinematicLimitFollowsBikeStep} checks the limit numerically. It uses stiff tires
and puts the mass at the rear axle, as the kinematic bicycle has it. Under the kinematic bicycle's
commands, the dynamic vehicle then stays within 0.011 (normalized units) of the kinematic path
over 200 ticks, with a mean path length of 5.6.

\subsection{Externally defined ports}
\label{sec:ports}
The form~\eqref{eq:plant-ph} accepts further ports. Suppose an external generalized force acts on
the body: a tow, wind, a contact, or a force defined by some other model. It enters through its
own input map, $\G_e u_e$, and has its own conjugate output $y_e=\G_e\T v$. The balance gains one
line, $y_e\T u_e$, which is booked as supplied power:
\begin{equation}
  \dot p=(\J-\R)v+\G u+\G_e u_e+f_\varepsilon-mg\theta e_x,\qquad
  \dot H=-v\T\R v+y\T u+y_e\T u_e+v\T f_\varepsilon-mg\theta v_x .
  \label{eq:ext-port}
\end{equation}
Nothing else changes: $\J$ and $\R$ are untouched, and passivity holds with the added supply. For a
force $F=(F_X,F_Y)$ given in the world frame and applied at the centre of gravity, the body
wrench is $(R(\psi)\T F,\,0)$. So
\begin{equation*}
  \G_e=\begin{pmatrix}R(\psi)\T\\0\quad0\end{pmatrix},\qquad y_e=R(\psi)\begin{pmatrix}v_x\\v_y\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation*}
and $y_e$ is the centre of gravity's world velocity. The supplied power is
$F\cdot\dot x_{\mathrm{cg}}$, as it should be. Applied at a body point $(\ell_x,\ell_y)$ instead,
the yaw row of $\G_e$ becomes $(-\ell_y,\ell_x)R(\psi)\T$, the moment arm. A port that stores
energy, such as a spring, belongs in $H$ instead.

\paragraph{Worked example: a navigation force.} GRL-SNAM and libcvc allow an external navigation
force $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$, with its spatial Jacobian. It might come from another field, a tow
or a learned term. It is joined to the navigator's conservative force (\code{ext\_in} in
\code{bike\_control}; \code{grl\_snam.ext\_rollout}, \code{material\_ext}), and it reaches the
two models differently.
\begin{itemize}
  \item On the navigator's point-mass surrogate, $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$ is a port force on the point
        mass. The per-step books (Section~\ref{sec:nav}) carry it as the port work
        $W_{\mathrm{port}}=\tfrac h2F_{\mathrm{ext}}\cdot(v+v')$. Left out, it is work of
        order $h$, against a bound of order $h^2$. The test
        \code{NavPotential.UnloggedForceRaisesTheAlarm} sees exactly that: 229 of 240 steps
        raise the alarm.
  \item On the vehicle, $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$ acts through the controller. It changes
        $(\delta_{\mathrm{cmd}},a_{\mathrm{cmd}})$, so it reaches the body only through the axle
        forces, which are already booked as $y\T u$, and through steering, which does no work. The
        plant's books need no new line: the external force's energy shows up as drive or brake.
        A model in which the force acts on the body directly, as a physical force, would add the
        column $\G_e$ above, with $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$ as $u_e$. libcvc does not implement such a
        column for $F_{\mathrm{ext}}$. Compliant contact (Section~\ref{sec:contact}) is one, applied
        at each point of the vehicle's outline.
\end{itemize}

\section{The discrete step and its energy books}
\label{sec:books}

\subsection{The step}
One step of length $h$ (\code{dyn\_plant}) from the commands $(\delta_{\mathrm{cmd}},a_{\mathrm{cmd}})$
and the grade has these parts:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Actuators.} The steering follows its command with an exact first-order lag,
        $1-e^{-h/\tau}$, with its change limited smoothly to a slew rate times $h$. The force
        follows its command with its own lag. Their outputs $(\delta_1,a_1)$ are the inputs the rest
        of the step applies.
  \item \emph{Loads and axle forces} from $a_1$, as in Section~\ref{sec:model}.
  \item \emph{Speed, predicted} from the axle forces, drag, rolling resistance and grade alone.
        Lateral forces are not read before step 4: in the stiff limit they are constraint forces.
  \item \emph{Lateral and yaw, implicit.} $(v_y,r)$ advance by implicit Euler at the predicted
        speed. The step is solved by a few Newton-like iterations, each linearizing the tires by
        their secants at its iterate. A sliding tire then pulls its slip toward zero, never past
        it, and stiff tires stay stable at the navigator's 0.06~s tick.
  \item \emph{Speed, corrected} with the front lateral force the implicit step applied, then
        \emph{clamped} to the vehicle's speed range.
  \item \emph{Low-speed blend.} Below a blend speed, a sigmoid in $\lvert v_x\rvert$ blends $(v_y,r)$
        toward the kinematic bicycle's, where slip angles mean nothing.
\end{enumerate}
Then the pose advances by the reconstruction $\dot q=T(q)v$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:ph}, at the new
heading and twist: $\psi'=\psi+hr'$, and the rear axle moves by $h$ times its velocity
$\big(v_x',\,v_y'-l_rr'\big)$ turned by $\psi'$, so $q'=q+h\,T(q')v'$. The pose does not enter
$H$, unless the side slope's option puts gravity there (Section~\ref{sec:full}). The step is first
order in $h$.

\subsection{The books}
\code{dyn\_plant\_books} runs exactly that step and returns, in the struct \code{dyn\_ph\_books},
\begin{equation}
  \Delta H=\underbrace{\widehat{\textstyle\int}y\T u}_{\text{supplied}}
  -\underbrace{\widehat{\textstyle\int}v\T\R v}_{\text{dissipated}}
  +\underbrace{\widehat{\textstyle\int}v\T f_\varepsilon}_{\text{floor}}
  \underbrace{-\widehat{\textstyle\int}mg\theta v_x}_{\text{grade}}
  +W_{\mathrm{lim}}+r_h .
  \label{eq:books}
\end{equation}
Each $\widehat\int$ is the trapezoid, over $h$, of that power. Its two ends are the step's start
(the start twist, with the inputs $(\delta_1,a_1)$ the step applied) and the twist the step reached
\emph{before} its limiters, the speed clamp and the low-speed blend. $W_{\mathrm{lim}}$ is the
kinetic energy those limiters then changed, booked exactly as the difference of $H$ across them.
$r_h$ is what is left, the integrator's residual. The step's arithmetic does not depend on the
books: \code{dyn\_plant} records the pre-limiter twist only when asked, and the books left
\code{DynBike.Golden}'s outputs unchanged.

\begin{proposition}[The books close to second order]
\label{prop:books}
Where the vector field is smooth along the step, $r_h=O(h^2)$ per step, so over a fixed horizon
$T$ the summed residual is $O(h)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Argument]
Before its limiters, the step is a consistent first-order method: implicit Euler on $(v_y,r)$,
and predicted-then-corrected Euler on $v_x$ with the same forces. So the pre-limiter twist differs
from the exact flow's twist at $t+h$ by $O(h^2)$. Along the exact flow,
$H(t+h)-H(t)=\int_t^{t+h}\dot H$ by~\eqref{eq:balance}, and the trapezoid of a smooth integrand
has error $O(h^3)$. Replacing the exact end state by the step's changes $H$ by $O(h^2)$ and the
trapezoid by $h\cdot O(h^2)$. The limiters' energy is booked exactly. Hence $r_h=O(h^2)$. The
finite number of Newton iterations, and the branch switches (load floor, saturation, the secant's
root branch), are part of $r_h$, not exceptions to it.
\end{proof}

\code{DynBikePH.StepBooksCloseToSecondOrder} checks this on a swerve past the tire's peak, with the
throttle working and the actuators lagging, at $\mu=0.6$. As $h$ halves from 0.02 to 0.0025~s,
the mean per-step $\lvert r_h\rvert$ falls by 3.83, 3.92 and 3.95. Over 6~s at $h=0.01$~s the
summed residual is $1.2\times10^{-3}$ of the energy that moved (the flow: the sum of the magnitudes
of every line). Braking hard to a stop in a turn makes the clamp and the blend act. The books charge
them (for example $-358$~J at $h=0.01$~s), and the rest closes to $4\times10^{-6}$ of the flow.

\subsection{Closing the books exactly}
\label{sec:dg}
\code{diff::dyn\_plant\_dg} (\code{inc/cvc/nav/detail/dyn\_bike\_dg.h}, libcvc \#978) is an opt-in
step whose books close exactly: the discrete gradient of~\eqref{eq:plant-ph}. $H=\tfrac12v\T Mv$
is quadratic in the twist, so its discrete gradient is the midpoint twist, and the step is the
implicit midpoint rule
\begin{equation}
  M\,\frac{v_1-v}{h}=\J(M\bar v)\,\bar v+Q(\bar v),\qquad \bar v=\tfrac12(v+v_1),
  \label{eq:dg}
\end{equation}
with $Q$ the tires, the axle forces, drag, rolling resistance and grade, taken at the midpoint
state with the actuators' outputs at the step's midpoint. Since $\J$ is skew,
$\Delta H=h\,\bar v\T Q(\bar v)$ holds line for line, and \code{dyn\_plant\_dg\_books} closes to
the tolerance of the Newton solve. The actuators, the speed clamp and the low-speed blend are
\code{dyn\_plant}'s, and the books charge the limiters as~\eqref{eq:books} does. \code{dyn\_plant}
and its callers are untouched, so the default is unchanged.
\begin{itemize}
  \item On the swerve of Proposition~\ref{prop:books}'s test, from $h=0.06$ to 0.0025~s, the
        residual is at most $1.7\times10^{-10}$~J a step, at most $1.3\times10^{-12}$ of the
        step's flow, where \code{dyn\_plant} leaves 25~J to 0.056~J a step.
  \item It is second order: its error at 2~s falls 3.92 to 4.03 times per halving of $h$, where
        \code{dyn\_plant}'s falls 1.98 to 2.13 times.
  \item No coasting step gains energy, at the navigator's 0.06~s step or at the live plant's six
        0.01~s substeps, with tires up to sixteen times stiffer than the vehicle's.
  \item It is A-stable but not L-stable. A tire much stiffer than the step can resolve swings its
        slip past zero and back, decaying, where \code{dyn\_plant}'s implicit Euler lands on the
        root in one step. At the live plant's substeps with the vehicle's own tires it swings no
        more than \code{dyn\_plant} does.
  \item A step costs 2.8 to 3.1~$\mu$s in double, about ten times \code{dyn\_plant}'s 0.30~$\mu$s.
\end{itemize}
It covers the twist without wheel spin, and it is C++ only: \code{pycvc} does not bind it, and
GRL-SNAM has no twin of it. Table~\ref{tab:val-dg} gives the gates.

\paragraph{With the wheels spinning.} \code{diff::dyn\_spin\_plant\_dg} (libcvc \#1045) extends the
step to the wheel-spin plant of Section~\ref{sec:spin}. Its $H$~\eqref{eq:spin} is quadratic in
$e=\nabla H=(v,\omega)$, and $Q$ is that plant's own generalized force
(\code{dyn\_spin\_ph\_forces\_at}). The body's rows take the midpoint, as in~\eqref{eq:dg}. The
wheels' slip is far stiffer than the step: its rate times the navigator's 0.06~s step is 60 to 600.
At the midpoint, which is A-stable but not L-stable, a disturbed slip, such as rims spun 1.5 times
the ground's speed, swings every step, and five substeps still swing 98, 36 and 8 times in 50
steps coasting from 0.5, 3 and 12~m/s. So the wheels' rows take the step's end:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal M\,\frac{e_1-e}{h}=\J(\mathcal M\tilde e)\,\tilde e+Q(\tilde e),\qquad
  \tilde e=e+\Theta(e_1-e),\qquad \Theta=\operatorname{diag}(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12,1,1),
  \label{eq:dg-spin}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal M=\operatorname{blockdiag}(M,J_f,J_r)$. Since $\J$ is skew,
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta H=h\,\tilde e\T Q(\tilde e)-\tfrac12\big(J_f\Delta\omega_f^2+J_r\Delta\omega_r^2\big)
\end{equation*}
exactly. The second term is the scheme's own damping, and \code{dyn\_spin\_plant\_dg\_books}
carries it on a line of its own, \code{scheme}, so the books still close to the solve's tolerance:
at most $1.3\times10^{-12}$ of the step's flow, where \code{dyn\_spin\_plant} leaves 20 to 240~J a
step at 0.06~s. The scheme's line is small on a swerve (21~J at 0.06~s) but not on a launch that
spins the wheels, where it takes $4.2\times10^{4}$~J against the slip's $4.4\times10^{5}$~J. The
wheels' speeds are first order, but at every step size the step is 10 to 13 times nearer its
converged solution than \code{dyn\_spin\_plant} is. Its slip swings about as often as
\code{dyn\_spin\_plant}'s, and no coasting step gains energy. A step costs 11 to 16~$\mu$s at
0.06~s, 19 to 27 times a \code{dyn\_spin\_plant} step. Like \code{dyn\_plant\_dg}, it is opt-in
and C++ only. Table~\ref{tab:val-dg-spin} gives the gates.

\subsection{The live plant}
\label{sec:live}
\code{cvc::physics::dyn\_bike\_plant} (libcvc \#833) makes the same vehicle a plant that a
navigation world (\code{cvc::nav::sim\_world}) drives. It sits beside the kinematic bicycle and
the rigid-body engine's vehicles, and needs no physics world. Agent $i$ drives one of a list of
vehicle descriptions, with parameters from \code{nav\_plant::dyn\_bike\_si\_of}, so the dynamic
and rigid-body plants read one description. Each tick of length $\Delta t$ runs
$\lceil\Delta t/h\rceil$ steps under the held command, with $h=0.01$~s by default (six steps at the
navigator's 0.06~s tick), in SI units. It books every step through \code{dyn\_plant\_books}. The
books are kept per tick (\code{last\_tick}) and since the agent was attached (\code{books}):
\begin{multline}
  K-K_0=\text{drive}-\text{brake}-\text{dissipated}-\text{slip}+\text{floor}+\text{grade}
  +\text{limiter}+\text{contact}\\
  -\text{stored}_{\mathrm c}-\text{dissipated}_{\mathrm c}+\text{exchange}_{\mathrm c}+\text{residual},
  \label{eq:live-books}
\end{multline}
where drive and brake are the positive and negative parts of each step's supplied term, split step
by step. The slip line and the three lines marked c are zero unless wheel spin or compliant contact
is on (Section~\ref{sec:ext}); with wheel spin, $K$ includes the wheels' spin. It is selected through \code{plant\_host} as \code{"dynamic"}, or
\code{"dynamic:<presets>"} to cycle vehicles. It is the plant that the coefficient trainer's
\code{rollout\_kind::dynamic} trains through, so a policy trained there is evaluated on its own
model.

\paragraph{Ground, world and driver.} When Table~\ref{tab:val-live}'s rows were measured, the plant
was flat (grade zero) and had no world to collide with, and the contact line was zero. libcvc
\#869 and \#873 add three things:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Ground.} Given a ground height (\code{plant.ground\_height}), the step takes $\theta$
        as the sine of the slope along the heading under the centre of gravity, over a wheelbase,
        and the grade line books it (Section~\ref{sec:grade}).
  \item \emph{World.} Given a rigid-body world (\code{dyn\_bike\_plant\_options::physics}), each
        chassis is a box in it, moved every substep as its model moved. A box that touches
        anything but the ground takes the world's pose and twist back into its model, and the
        energy that changes is booked as contact. This contact is outside the pH form: it
        replaces the state rather than entering through $H$, $\R$ or a port
        (Section~\ref{sec:review}); compliant contact (Section~\ref{sec:contact}) is the
        alternative inside it. With Jolt in the build, \code{plant\_host} now builds that world
        for \code{"dynamic"}, holding the scene's ground and buildings.
  \item \emph{Driver.} The navigator's acceleration goes through the rigid-body plant's driver,
        on by default (\code{dyn\_bike\_plant\_options::driver}). It holds the vehicle on its
        brakes at a standstill and corrects the drive force for the slope and for what the last
        substep fell short by.
\end{itemize}
The four-wheel vehicle is a live plant in the same way, with its grade force on: libcvc \#869's
\code{dyn\_4w\_plant}, which \code{plant\_host} selects as \code{"dynamic4w"}.
Table~\ref{tab:val-live-world} gives the tests of these additions. Three more options of the single
track's live plant are off by default (Section~\ref{sec:ext}): \code{wheel\_spin};
\code{side\_slope}, which reads the same ground height; and \code{contact}, compliant contact
against a field (\code{contact\_field}) and between the vehicles. Compliant contact takes the
world's place: a plant uses one or the other.

\section{The four-wheel vehicle}
\label{sec:four-wheel}

libcvc's two-track vehicle, \code{diff::dyn4\_plant} (\code{inc/cvc/nav/detail/dyn\_4w.h}, twinned
as \code{grl\_snam.dyn4}), has four contact patches with Ackermann front wheels, a spring and a
damper at each corner, and heave, roll and pitch. It leaves out the unsprung masses, camber and
the anti-roll bars, and has wheel spin only by option (Section~\ref{sec:spin}). libcvc \#850 writes it in pH form at every slip, with per-step
books, as Sections~\ref{sec:ph} and~\ref{sec:books} do for the single track. The plant's arithmetic
does not depend on either: \code{Dyn4.Golden} and \code{Dyn4ExtRollout.Golden} pass unchanged.

\paragraph{State and structure.} The state is $x=(q,p)$, nine coordinates: $q=(z,\phi,\vartheta)$
is the body's heave, roll and pitch from rest, and $p=M\xi$ the momenta of its twist
$\xi=(v_x,v_y,r,\dot z,\dot\phi,\dot\vartheta)$, with $M=\operatorname{diag}(m,m,I_z,m,I_x,I_y)$.
The pose, the rear axle's as in the single track, follows from the twist by the same
reconstruction $\dot q=T(q)\,(v_x,v_y,r)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:ph}), and enters only through the
ground under the wheels. With $\nabla H=(\partial H/\partial
q,\,\xi)$ and $u=(F_{x,1},\dots,F_{x,4})$ the wheels' drive or brake forces,
\begin{equation}
  H=\tfrac12\xi\T M\xi+\textstyle\sum_i\tfrac12k_il_i^2+mgz,\qquad
  \dot x=(\J-\R)\nabla H+\G u+f_{\mathrm{lever}},\qquad y=\G\T\nabla H,
  \label{eq:4w}
\end{equation}
where $l_i$ is corner $i$'s spring compression; the springs' preloads hold the body at $q=0$ on
flat ground. This is the default plant, without the grade force described below.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\J=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&I\\0&\J_h(p_h)&0\\-I&0&0\end{pmatrix}$ over $(q,p_h,p_v)$, the
        horizontal and vertical momenta: canonical in the vertical, and the Lie--Poisson
        structure~\eqref{eq:J} in the horizontal.
  \item $\R=\operatorname{blockdiag}\big(0,\;c_xe_xe_x\T+\sum_iS_in_in_i\T,\;\sum_ic'_ic_ic_i\T\big)$.
        Here $n_i=(-\sin d_i,\cos d_i,x_i\cos d_i+y_i\sin d_i)$ carries wheel $i$'s lateral force,
        at its offset $(x_i,y_i)$ and angle $d_i$, onto $(v_x,v_y,r)$. So $n_i\T v$ is the lateral
        velocity $w_i$ in that wheel's frame, where its slip is measured, and $S_i=-F_{y,i}/w_i\ge0$
        is its secant (Lemma~\ref{lem:secant}). The last block is the dampers: $c'_i$ is corner
        $i$'s damping and $c_i=(1,y_i,-x_i)$ takes $\dot q$ to its vertical rate. So $\R$ is
        positive semi-definite at every slip and every steering angle.
  \item $\G$'s columns are $T_i=(\cos d_i,\sin d_i,x_i\sin d_i-y_i\cos d_i,0,h\sin d_i,-h\cos d_i)$
        on the twist: wheel $i$'s force along the wheel, at its contact patch, $h$ below the centre
        of gravity. So $y_i$ is that patch's speed along its wheel, roll and pitch included, and
        $y\T u$ is the forces' whole power. Steering is not an input: the angles $d_i$ only turn
        $n_i$ and $T_i$.
  \item $f_\varepsilon=0$. Each slip is in its wheel's frame and rooted at $w_i=0$, and the form
        takes the exact secant, so nothing is left at the speed floor.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The lever.} One term fits neither $\J$ nor $\R$:
\begin{equation*}
  f_{\mathrm{lever}}=\big(0,\;h\textstyle\sum_iF_{y,i}\cos d_i,\;h\sum_iF_{y,i}\sin d_i\big)
  \quad\text{on }(p_z,p_\phi,p_\vartheta),
\end{equation*}
the moments of the tires' lateral forces on roll and pitch. The slips do
not see the body's roll and pitch rates (a patch's lateral velocity has no $h\dot\phi$ in it), so
the coupling runs one way. It is neither skew nor dissipative, and it is booked on its own line,
$\xi\T f_{\mathrm{lever}}$, which vanishes with $h$. So
\begin{equation}
  \dot H=-\nabla H\T\R\nabla H+y\T u+\xi\T f_{\mathrm{lever}}+\textstyle\sum_ik_il_i\,\dot h_{g,i},
  \label{eq:4w-balance}
\end{equation}
and the vehicle is passive with respect to its wheels' forces except for the lever. The last term
is the ground under wheel $i$, at height $h_{g,i}$, lifting its spring as the wheel rolls over it;
it is zero on flat ground. The lever is small: over 8~s of swerving past the tires' peak on the
tests' full-size preset at $\mu=0.6$, it books about 1.0~kJ against 277~kJ dissipated.

\paragraph{Grade.} By default the plant has no grade force on its horizontal motion. The terrain's
height enters only the springs, whose bases it moves, and $z$ is heave from rest, not elevation. A
vehicle then climbs a hill for free, and the books show that energy as the ground line
of~\eqref{eq:4w-balance}, from outside. The opt-in \code{dyn4\_params::grade\_force} adds each
spring's horizontal reaction on the slope under its wheel, $f_{\mathrm{grade}}$, on $(v_x,v_y,r)$.
It is minus the gradient of the springs' energy in the pose, through the ground under the wheels.
With the pose in the state, $T^*SE(2)$'s canonical structure puts it in $\J$, and the terrain's
energy is in $H$; libcvc \#987 writes that form out and checks it (Section~\ref{sec:full}). Its power, booked as a grade line, cancels the ground line:
$\xi\T f_{\mathrm{grade}}=-\sum_ik_il_i\dot h_{g,i}$. Coasting up a 5\% ramp for 2~s from 10~m/s,
with drag, rolling resistance and the dampers off, the vehicle keeps its speed without the force
while $H$ gains $mg$ times the climb. With it, it loses 0.986~m/s, against $g\,s\,t=0.981$, and $H$
holds to within 0.8\%. The option is off by default, so the plant's goldens and GRL-SNAM's
\code{dyn4.py} twin are unchanged; the twin does not have it.

\paragraph{The books.} \code{dyn4\_plant\_books} runs \code{dyn4\_plant}'s step, to the last bit, and
returns
\begin{equation}
  \Delta H=\text{supplied}-\text{dissipated}+\text{lever}+\text{grade}+\text{ground}+W_{\mathrm{lim}}+r_h .
  \label{eq:4w-books}
\end{equation}
As in~\eqref{eq:books}, each power is the trapezoid between the step's start and its pre-limiter
state, both on the ground the step stood on, and $W_{\mathrm{lim}}$ is the limiters' change of $H$.
The ground line is the springs' energy that the new ground under the wheels adds at the step's end.
The step holds the ground and the wheels' loads at its start; that is integrator error, so it lands
in $r_h$. On an 8~s weave at 0.2~Hz to near full lock at $\mu=0.6$, with a tire past its peak in
about 27\% of the steps, $\lvert r_h\rvert$ falls 3.84 to 3.98 times per halving of $h$ from 0.02 to
0.0025~s, flat and over hills. At $h=0.01$~s the summed residual is $5.0\times10^{-4}$ of the flow
on flat ground and $1.0\times10^{-3}$ over hills. With the grade force on, the grade and ground
lines cancel to second order: their per-step sum falls 4.0 times per halving.
Table~\ref{tab:val-4w} gives the gates.

\section{Wheel spin, the side slope and compliant contact}
\label{sec:ext}

Three opt-in extensions answer two of the gaps raised in review (Section~\ref{sec:review}) and the
side slope that Section~\ref{sec:grade} leaves out: wheel spin (libcvc \#982,
Section~\ref{sec:spin}), the full $(q,p)$ form with gravity in $H$ (\#987, Section~\ref{sec:full}),
and compliant contact (\#983, Section~\ref{sec:contact}). Each is off by default, and with it off
every plant's arithmetic is as before: the goldens of Tables~\ref{tab:val-plant}--\ref{tab:val-4w}
pass unchanged. CVC-Lab/GRL-SNAM \#146 twins all three (Section~\ref{sec:grl}).

\subsection{Wheel spin}
\label{sec:spin}
In the plants of Sections~\ref{sec:model} and~\ref{sec:four-wheel}, each axle's drive or brake
force is set within its grip, so no wheel spins and none locks. The opt-in plants
\code{diff::dyn\_spin\_plant} and \code{diff::dyn4\_spin\_plant} give each axle's wheels (on four
wheels, each wheel) a speed $\omega$ of its own, with a spin inertia and the rolling radius $r_w$.

\paragraph{The model.} The actuator's $a$ becomes torques, split between the axles as the force
was. For $a\ge0$ the drive's torque on axle $w$, $T_{d,w}$, is $r_w$ times that axle's share of
$ma$. For $a<0$ the brakes' torque $T_{b,w}$ is $r_w$ times its share of $m\lvert a\rvert$, and it
acts against the rolling, as $-T_{b,w}\operatorname{sg}(\omega_w)$, with $\operatorname{sg}$ the
sign smoothed over the speed floor at the rim. A brake stops a wheel and holds it, so a negative
$a$ never drives backward. Each tire pushes along its wheel, $t_w$ of~\eqref{eq:dirs}, with
Pacejka's longitudinal force of its slip:
\begin{equation}
  F_{x,w}=D_w\sin\!\big(C_x\arctan(B_x\sigma_w/u_w)\big),\qquad \sigma_w=r_w\omega_w-t_w\T v,
  \label{eq:spin-tire}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_w$ is the contact patch's slip velocity (the rim's speed less the ground's along the
wheel), $u_w$ the ground's speed along the wheel, floored as in~\eqref{eq:slips}, and
$D_w=\mu F_{z,w}$ the axle's whole grip. The lateral force takes what grip the friction ellipse
leaves, and the wheel feels $-r_wF_{x,w}$. On the tests' full-size preset the wheels' radius is
0.47~m, their inertia 12~kg\,m$^2$ an axle, and the longitudinal peak, taken from the rigid-body
simulator's description of the same vehicle, at slip ratio 0.080.

\paragraph{The form.} With the wheels' momenta $L=(J_f\omega_f,J_r\omega_r)$ in the state,
$x=(p,L)$ has five coordinates, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  H=\tfrac12v\T Mv+\tfrac12\big(J_f\omega_f^2+J_r\omega_r^2\big),\qquad \nabla H=(v,\omega),\\
  \dot x=(\J-\R)\nabla H+\G u+f_\varepsilon-mg\theta\,e_x,\qquad
  u=T_d-T_b\operatorname{sg}(\omega),\qquad y=\G\T\nabla H=\omega,
\end{gathered}
  \label{eq:spin}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\J=\operatorname{blockdiag}(\J(p),0)$: the structure couples nothing to the wheels;
  \item $\R=\operatorname{blockdiag}(\R_{\mathrm{body}},0)+\sum_wK_wd_wd_w\T$. Here
        $\R_{\mathrm{body}}$ is~\eqref{eq:R} with each tire's lateral grip what its longitudinal
        force leaves; $d_f=(-t_f,r_w,0)$ and $d_r=(-t_r,0,r_w)$, so that $d_w\T\nabla H=\sigma_w$;
        and $K_w=F_{x,w}/\sigma_w$ is the longitudinal force's secant through its root;
  \item $\G=[\,e_4\;\;e_5\,]$: each axle's torque on its wheels.
\end{itemize}
A Pacejka force with $C_x<2$ has its slip's sign, so $K_w\ge0$ by the argument of
Lemma~\ref{lem:secant}, and $\R$ is positive semi-definite at every slip, rolling, spinning or
locked, and at any steering angle. The port is now (wheel torque, wheel speed), and $y\T u$ is
shaft power. Along solutions
\begin{equation}
  \dot H=-v\T\R_{\mathrm{body}}v-\textstyle\sum_wK_w\sigma_w^2+\omega\T T_d
  -\sum_wT_{b,w}\,\omega_w\operatorname{sg}(\omega_w)+v\T f_\varepsilon-mg\theta\,v_x ,
  \label{eq:spin-balance}
\end{equation}
in which the brakes' term is non-positive at every state. Steering still does no work. On four
wheels, $x=(q,p,L)$ has 13 coordinates, $H$ keeps the springs and $mgz$ of~\eqref{eq:4w}, and $t_i$
is $\G$'s column $T_i$ of Section~\ref{sec:four-wheel}: the patch's speed along its wheel, roll and
pitch included. So the longitudinal coupling is reciprocal and goes into $\R$ whole, with no
one-way term like the lateral forces' lever.

\paragraph{The step and its books.} The step advances $(v_x,\omega_f,\omega_r)$ by implicit Euler,
with the tires and the brakes linearized by their secants through their roots. The matrix it solves
is symmetric positive definite, so the solve never fails, the stiff slip stays stable at the
navigator's step, and a spinning or locking wheel's slip is pulled toward zero rather than past it.
\code{dyn\_spin\_plant\_books} and \code{dyn4\_spin\_plant\_books} book each step as
in~\eqref{eq:books} and~\eqref{eq:4w-books}, with the brakes and the longitudinal slip on lines of
their own:
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta H=\text{supplied}-\text{braked}-\text{dissipated}-\text{slip}+\text{floor}+\text{grade}
  +W_{\mathrm{lim}}+r_h
\end{equation*}
on the single track, plus the lever and the ground on four wheels. The live plant takes wheel spin
by option (\code{dyn\_bike\_plant\_options::wheel\_spin}), and its books gain the slip line.

\paragraph{What it does.} With full drive on grip 0.3 for 1~s from 5~m/s, the front wheels run at
slip ratio 3.57 and the rear at 1.76. Braking at 8~m/s$^2$ from 20~m/s on grip 0.6 locks the front
wheels (slip ratio $-0.97$), and the vehicle stops in 34.8~m, where \code{dyn\_plant} stops in
33.9~m. With the wheels' inertia at 1\% of the preset's, the plant stays within 2~mm of
\code{dyn\_plant} over 5~s of weaving. With the preset's wheels, a 0.8~m/s$^2$ drive for 2~s from
10~m/s ends at 11.5369~m/s, where the effective mass $m+\sum J/r_w^2$ predicts 11.5376. The books
close to second order on a swerve past the lateral peak, and faster than that on a launch that spins
the wheels to slip ratio 8.7 and then locks them, while the slip mode is stiff against $h$.
Table~\ref{tab:val-spin} gives the gates.

\subsection{The full form and the side slope}
\label{sec:full}
Proposition~\ref{prop:ph} puts the pose in the state, but its $H$ does not depend on the pose: the
grade is data, and acts along the heading only (Section~\ref{sec:grade}). libcvc \#987 adds the
slope across the heading, with gravity in $H$. With $h_g(x,y)$ the ground's height and
$c(q)=(x+l_r\cos\psi,\,y+l_r\sin\psi)$ the centre of gravity of the rear-axle pose $q$, let
$V(q)=mg\,h_g(c(q))$ and $H=\tfrac12p\T M^{-1}p+V(q)$. The structure of~\eqref{eq:plant-full} is
unchanged, and its rows become
\begin{equation}
  \dot q=T(q)v,\qquad \dot p=-T(q)\T\nabla_qV+\big(\J(p)-\R\big)v+\G u+f_\varepsilon,
  \label{eq:plant-ground}
\end{equation}
with $-T(q)\T\nabla_qV=-mg(\theta_\parallel,\theta_\perp,0)$, where $\theta_\parallel$ and
$\theta_\perp$ are the ground's gradient at the centre of gravity along and across the heading.
Gravity moves from the supply into $H$, and its power, $-\dot V=-mg(\theta_\parallel v_x+
\theta_\perp v_y)$, is the grade line. In code these are \code{dyn\_ph\_T}, \code{dyn\_ph\_J\_full}
and \code{dyn\_ph\_rhs\_full}, with the ground overloads \code{dyn\_ph\_potential} and
\code{dyn\_ph\_grad\_q}.

\paragraph{Tangents, not sines.} $\theta_\parallel$ and $\theta_\perp$ are the gradient's
components, the slope's tangents. The model's pose and twist are the vehicle's projections on the
plane, so the force whose work is $-\Delta V$ is $-mg\nabla h_g$. The sine, which the momentum-only
form takes as its $\theta$, leaves $mg(\tan-\sin)\approx mg\theta^3/2$ unbooked, and a smaller step
does not remove it. At a 10\% grade the two differ by 0.5\%.

\paragraph{The plant.} \code{dyn\_plant} takes an optional slope across the heading, whose force
$-mg\theta_\perp$ joins the lateral solve. \code{dyn\_plant\_ground} samples both slopes from a
ground under the centre of gravity, and \code{dyn\_plant\_books\_ground} books the grade line as
$-\Delta V$ over the step, so the kinetic $\Delta H$ less the grade line is $\Delta(T+V)$. The
wheel-spin plant has the same (\code{dyn\_spin\_plant\_ground}), and the live plant takes it by
option (\code{dyn\_bike\_plant\_options::side\_slope}), reading the ground height of
Section~\ref{sec:live}. The step's pose update is $q_1=q_0+h\,T(q_1)v_1$ to round-off: the
reconstruction of Proposition~\ref{prop:ph}, taken at the step's end.

\paragraph{What it shows.} Closed loop across a 15\% hillside with swells, with the body slipping
up to 0.052~rad, for 6~s: with $V$ in $H$ the books close to second order, the residual falling 4.0
times per halving of $h$, and their sum is $2.8\times10^{-5}$ of the flow at $h=2.5$~ms. On the same
steps, the momentum-only books leave $1.5$--$1.6\times10^{-2}$ of the flow unbooked at every $h$,
the plant without the side slope $1.2$--$1.5\times10^{-3}$, and the sines $1.8$--$2.1\times10^{-3}$:
model gaps, not integrator error. The live plant, driven straight across a 10\% side slope for
6~s, drifts 0.196~m downhill, and its grade line equals $-mg\Delta z$ to round-off.

\paragraph{Four wheels.} The four-wheel plant has no side-slope gap: its grade force acts along and
across the heading, and the terrain's energy is in $H$ through the springs
(Section~\ref{sec:four-wheel}). What it lacked was the pose in the state.
\code{dyn4\_ph\_rhs\_full} adds it, for twelve coordinates in all, and with it
$-T\T\partial H/\partial g$, $g$ the pose, is the grade force to round-off. So the grade force moves
into the structure, as Section~\ref{sec:four-wheel} says it does. Without the grade force, the
plant's momentum rates on the tests' hillside miss up to 0.20 of the full form's.
Table~\ref{tab:val-full} gives the gates.

\subsection{Compliant contact}
\label{sec:contact}
The live plant's rigid-body world (Section~\ref{sec:live}) resolves a collision by replacing the
model's state with the world's. libcvc \#983 adds contact inside the single track's form, as an
option of the live plant (\code{dyn\_bike\_plant\_options::contact}) in place of that world,
against the scene and between vehicles (\code{inc/cvc/nav/detail/dyn\_contact.h}).

\paragraph{The model.} A vehicle's outline about its centre of gravity, its chassis box and wheels,
is sampled at points $l_k$: by default seven along each side and four along each end. Let $\phi$ be
the scene's signed distance, positive in free space, read from the navigation world's own grid
(\code{dyn\_bike\_plant::sdf\_field}) or from any sampler, and $d_k=\phi(P_k)$ at point $k$'s world
position $P_k$. Within a skin $s$ of an obstacle, each point stores a barrier energy
$\psi_k=\tfrac12k(s-d_k)^2$. It damps its normal velocity with the Hunt--Crossley coefficient
$c_k=c\,(s-d_k)/s$, which is zero at first touch, so a point that leaves is never held back. And it
slides along the obstacle with Coulomb friction on the barrier's push, its direction smoothed. Each
point's world force $F_k$ reaches the body through the external port of Section~\ref{sec:ports} at
its arm: $\G_{e,k}\T F_k=\big(R(\psi)\T F_k,\;l_k\times R(\psi)\T F_k\big)$.

\paragraph{The form.} With the pose $g$ of the centre of gravity in the state, as in
Section~\ref{sec:full},
\begin{equation}
  H=\tfrac12v\T Mv+\Phi(g),\qquad \Phi=\textstyle\sum_k\psi_k,\qquad
  \R_c=\textstyle\sum_k\big(c_k\,b_kb_k\T+f_k\,a_ka_k\T\big),
  \label{eq:contact}
\end{equation}
with $b_k=\G_{e,k}\T\nabla\phi_k$ the point's normal and $a_k=\G_{e,k}\T t_k$ its tangent on the
twist, and $f_k$ the friction's secant through zero sliding. The barrier's force,
$-T_c\T\nabla_g\Phi$ with $T_c=R(\psi)\oplus1$, comes through the canonical structure, as gravity's
does in~\eqref{eq:plant-ground}. The damping and friction are $-\R_cv$, and $\R_c$ is positive
semi-definite. So contact stores energy in $H$ and dissipates it in $\R$; it supplies none.

\paragraph{The plant and its books.} The plant applies the contact's wrench as the external port,
held over each substep, and books its power $y_e\T u_e$. The contact's books split that into the
barrier's change and the damper's work. The barrier is held from the substep's start while the step
moves the pose with its new twist, so its work differs from its change by second order per step.
The damper and the friction are implicit (libcvc \#1004, \code{diff::dyn\_contact\_implicit}): the
step solves $(M+h\R_c)\,v_d=Mv_0+hW_c$, with $M=\operatorname{diag}(m,m,I_z)$ and $W_c$ the
barrier's wrench, and holds $W_d=-\R_cv_d$, taking the friction's secant again at the sliding speed
$v_d$ reaches. So $W_d\T v_d\le0$, and without the barrier the step never raises the kinetic
energy, at any $h$. Touching vehicles are solved together, so their forces stay equal and
opposite. A substep in which a point would close more than \code{contact\_step\_depth} (0.1~m by
default) on what it meets is cut into equal pieces, until nothing touches or closes. The books
then close to second order through fast impacts at the default 0.01~s substep.

Before \#1004 the damper and friction were held from the substep's start. Their coefficients grow
with the penetration, so past some depth $h\R_c/m$ exceeded 2 and they reversed the approach
within a step. Driven by the navigator into a wall at 7.8~m/s at 0.01~s, the full-size preset
then rebounded at 3.2~m/s, the books showed the contact supplying $1.5\times10^{5}$~J, and
$3.1\times10^{-2}$ of the flow went unbooked. With \#1004 the same impact dissipates
$8.16\times10^{4}$~J in the contact, against $8.19\times10^{4}$~J at a 0.00125~s substep. The
speed clamp takes nothing, the slowest speed is $-0.47$~m/s, as at the fine step, and the books
leave $1.3\times10^{-3}$ of the flow. The navigation world's own field made the overshoot worse.
Its grid signed distance runs from $+1$ to $-1$ cell across the cell at an obstacle's face, so
$\lvert\nabla\phi\rvert=2$ there. Against it, the barrier is four times and the damper eight times
as stiff per metre as against an exact wall. Inside a wall two cells thick the field is flat at
$-1$ cell, so a vehicle that got half a cell in would feel no barrier.

Between two vehicles, each one's points meet the
other's outline, a rounded rectangle's signed distance, both ways. Each force acts on both bodies,
equal and opposite at the point, so the pair keeps its momentum. The barrier's energy depends only
on the pair's relative pose, and it is shared half to each vehicle's books, as the damper's power
is. The live books~\eqref{eq:live-books} carry three contact lines: the barrier's stored change,
the contact's dissipation, and the exchange, what the vehicles passed each other through their
contacts. Summed over a pair, the exchange is the integrator's error alone.

\paragraph{Against the rigid-body world.} Coasting from 5~m/s into a wall head on, the full-size
preset rebounds from the compliant barrier with 0.19 of its approach speed; the rigid-body world's
box keeps none of it. At $30^\circ$ to the wall, the rigid-body world's friction yaws the vehicle
$2.8^\circ$ into the wall, where the compliant plant turns $0.3^\circ$ the other way, and turns
$0.5^\circ$ toward the wall with its friction raised from 0.2 to 0.6. Into a parked vehicle of the
same preset, the compliant plant leaves the two at 1.90 and 2.88~m/s, and the rigid-body world at
2.27 and 2.28~m/s. The compliant contact is the more elastic.

\paragraph{Learning from contact.} \code{cut\_flights} with \code{keep\_contact} (\code{cvc
plant-log --keep-contact}) keeps the windows with a contact or an impact, with a last column that
counts the contacts each interval began. A learned plant can read it as a port or a modulation and
so fit the mean response of the intervals in contact. It cannot learn where or when a contact
happens, nor its direction: the rows carry no obstacle. Tables~\ref{tab:val-contact}
and~\ref{tab:val-contact-plant} give the gates.

\section{The learned plant: \texorpdfstring{\code{cvc::ph}}{cvc::ph}}
\label{sec:learned}

\code{cvc::ph::se2\_vehicle} (\code{inc/cvc/ph/se2\_vehicle.h}) is a native C++ port of an
$SE(2)$ vehicle model built on the stochastic port-Hamiltonian Gaussian processes of McLennan,
Rapolu and Bajaj~\cite{liesphgp2026}. Their JAX code, \code{CVC-Lab/LieSPHGP}, models an $SO(3)$
pendulum, $SE(3)$ quadrotors and an $SE(3)$ underwater vehicle. It has no $SE(2)$ vehicle.

The port follows a separate $SE(2)$ vehicle package built on that code at commit \code{65db994}.
That package is not part of the public repository. libcvc's golden for the port
(\code{ph\_se2\_golden.json}) was generated once from it, and its provenance records
``\code{CVC-Lab/LieSPHGP @ 65db994 + the SE(2) package}''. The package follows LieSPHGP's
underwater-vehicle model reduced to $SE(2)$, with two differences: its input map reads the twist,
where LieSPHGP's reads the pose, and its inputs are a vehicle's drive and steering.

The model contains no physical constant: everything is learned from logged runs of a vehicle. In
libcvc those runs come from \code{cvc::physics::plant\_log}. It records the vehicles of a navigation
world driving the rigid-body engine's plant (\code{cvc::physics::nav\_plant}, on Jolt Physics).

\subsection{Model}
The state is $(x,y,\psi,v)$ with $v=(v_x,v_y,r)$. The inputs $u$ are the logged commands divided
by their training RMS. With $p=Mv$ and $H=\tfrac12p\T M^{-1}p+V(x,y)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
  d(x,y)=R(\psi)(v_x,v_y)\,dt,\qquad d\psi=r\,dt,\qquad dv=M^{-1}dp+\Sigma\,dW,\\
  dp=\Big(\operatorname{ad}^*_vp-\begin{pmatrix}R(\psi)\T\nabla V\\0\end{pmatrix}-D(v)\,v+G(v)\,u\Big)dt,
\end{gathered}
  \label{eq:learned}
\end{equation}
with $\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_3)$. Each piece is a learned level plus a
Gaussian-process residual:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $M^{-1}=\operatorname{spd}(\ell_M)$, constant: a rigid body's inertia.
  \item $D(v)=\operatorname{spd}\big(\ell_D+\mathrm{GP}_D(v/s_v)\big)$, positive definite at every
        twist: it can only dissipate.
  \item $G(v)=\ell_G+\mathrm{GP}_G(v/s_v)$, a $3\times n_u$ input map.
  \item $V(x,y)=\ell_V\cdot(x,y)+\mathrm{GP}_V\big((x,y)/\lambda_V\big)$, the potential.
\end{itemize}
Here $s_v$ is the RMS of each twist channel over the training data. Here
$\operatorname{spd}(\ell)=LL\T$, where $L$ is lower triangular with diagonal $e^{\ell_1},e^{\ell_2},e^{\ell_3}$
and off-diagonal $\ell_4,\ell_5,\ell_6$ (\code{spd\_from\_levels}, \code{mat3.h}), so the result is
symmetric positive definite for every value of the six levels. Only $M^{-1}D$, $M^{-1}G$ and
$M^{-1}\nabla V$ are identifiable from trajectories, plus $M$'s shape through
$\operatorname{ad}^*_vp$.

\subsection{Gaussian processes on random Fourier features}
Each GP is $\mathrm{GP}(z)=\phi(z)\T W$ on $n$ random Fourier features~\cite{rahimi2007} of a
Mat\'ern-$\nu$ kernel with length scale $\lambda$ (\code{inc/cvc/ph/rff.h}):
\begin{equation}
  \phi_k(z)=\sqrt{2/n}\,\cos(\omega_k\cdot z+b_k),\qquad
  \omega_k=\frac{w_k}{\lambda\sqrt{s_k/(2\nu)}},\quad w_k\sim\mathcal N(0,I),\;s_k\sim\chi^2_{2\nu},\;
  b_k\sim\mathcal U(0,2\pi).
  \label{eq:rff}
\end{equation}
These are the Student-$t$ spectral samples of the Mat\'ern kernel. Every derivative is bounded and
in closed form, which the reverse pass uses. The weights have a mean-field Gaussian posterior,
$q(W)=\mathcal N(\text{mean},\operatorname{diag}e^{2\log\text{std}})$. The defaults are $n=64$,
$\nu=5/2$ and $\lambda=1$. The library's own generator (xoshiro256** with written-out normal and
gamma draws) draws the same features from a seed on every compiler.

\subsection{Energy balance, output and noise power}
\label{sec:learned-energy}
Along the drift of~\eqref{eq:learned}, $\operatorname{ad}^*_vp$ does no work, and the potential's
force returns exactly what moving through $V$ costs. So
\begin{equation}
  \dot H=-v\T D(v)\,v+y\T u,\qquad y=G(v)\T v .
  \label{eq:learned-balance}
\end{equation}
\code{output()} returns $y$, so $y\T u$ is the supplied power. The noise is added to the twist,
so it supplies energy too, on average. Since $dH=v\T M\,dv+\tfrac12dv\T M\,dv$ (plus the position
terms) and $\Sigma$ is constant, so the It\^o and Stratonovich forms agree,
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb E[dH]=\Big(-v\T Dv+y\T u+\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\big(\Sigma\T M\Sigma\big)\Big)dt .
  \label{eq:noise-power}
\end{equation}
\code{noise\_power()} returns $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma\T M\Sigma)$. Pathwise, the drift alone is
passive. The diffusion's power is booked as its own line, not attributed to $D$.

\subsection{The step}
One step of size $h$ is ``Lie-IMEX'': the damping is implicit and everything else is explicit. With
$e=M^{-1}(\operatorname{ad}^*_vp-[R\T\nabla V;0]+Gu)$ and $K=M^{-1}D(v)$ evaluated at each stage,
and the twist's noise increment $\eta=\sqrt h\,\Sigma z$,
\begin{align}
  (I+hK_1)\,v_p&=v+h\,e_1+\eta, &(x,y)_p&=(x,y)+hR(\psi)(v_x,v_y), \qquad \psi_p=\psi+hr,\notag\\
  (I+\tfrac h2K_2)\,v'&=v+\tfrac h2(e_1+e_2-K_1v)+\eta, &(x,y)'&=(x,y)+\tfrac h2\big(R(\psi)v_{xy}+R(\psi_p)v_{p,xy}\big),
  \label{eq:imex}
\end{align}
and $\psi'=\psi+\tfrac h2(r+r_p)$. It is a Heun predictor--corrector with implicit damping, and so
second order. Since $SO(2)$ is abelian, the heading stays a continuous angle without a
group-valued update. It is not structure-preserving and keeps no discrete energy identity. The
step is templated on the scalar, so the same code runs in float and in double.

\subsection{Training}
\code{cvc::ph::se2\_rollout\_objective} (\code{inc/cvc/ph/se2\_train.h}) is the rollout loss of the
$SE(2)$ package's trainer:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L=\operatorname{rollout}(\text{windows}\mid W)+\beta\,\frac{\mathrm{KL}(q\,\Vert\,p)}{6N_{\mathrm{obs}}},
  \qquad W=\text{mean}+e^{\log\text{std}}z,\;z\sim\mathcal N(0,I).
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
From each window's first sample, the model is rolled out under the recorded inputs and compared
with every later sample (position, wrapped heading and twist): the mean over the window of the
squared error summed over the six channels, averaged over the windows that stay finite. The
objective takes one reparameterized weight draw per step. $\beta$ rises linearly to its maximum
over an annealing period, and $N_{\mathrm{obs}}$ counts the training windows times their
intervals. The gradient is a hand-written reverse pass through every step of the window: the
$3\times3$ implicit solves, the SPD parameterization, the random-feature GPs, and $\nabla V$'s own
derivative in position. The optimizer is LieSPHGP's:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the whole gradient is clipped to global norm 1;
  \item Adam, with a cosine-decayed learning rate, updates every leaf except the levels: the GP
        weights, and the noise scales, although the rollout objective gives the noise scales no
        gradient (below);
  \item Prodigy, one per subnetwork, updates the levels.
\end{itemize}
The command-line tool \code{cvc ph-train} fits a model to plant-log runs and writes it as JSON
(format \code{cvc-ph-se2/1}).

The rollouts in this objective run the drift alone, with no noise increment. So the objective fits
$M^{-1}$, $D$, $G$ and $V$, but $\Sigma$ gets no gradient from it, and there is no observation
model. LieSPHGP's own trainers fit those with an error-state extended Kalman filter: they minimize
the filter's negative log marginal likelihood plus the KL term, a negative ELBO, which learns $\Sigma$
and the observation noise. That stage is not yet ported. The model format already carries the
observation-noise parameters it will need.

\subsection{Steering: a port in the reference form, a modulation by option}
\label{sec:learned-steer}
\paragraph{The inconsistency.} In the reference form every input is a port. In libcvc's plant logs
the inputs are the vehicle's drive and its steering (\code{plant\_log.h}), so the supply $y\T u$
contains a steering term, $y_\delta u_\delta$. Section~\ref{sec:steer} shows that the vehicle's
steering does no work on its body, and nothing in the learned structure forces that term to
vanish. With its GP weights zero, the reference form reduces exactly to the linear-tire bicycle
with steering as an input (\code{PhSe2.ReducesToTheLinearTireBicycle}), which is the truncation of
Section~\ref{sec:steer}.

\paragraph{The diagnostic.} Split $y\T u$ by input channel, using \code{output()}. Two measurements
(transfix/libcvc \#851):
\begin{itemize}
  \item On the golden fit, steering is 13.4\% of $\lvert y\T u\rvert$ at its 16 states. With the drive
        input at zero, steering alone supplies energy at 10 of them
        (\code{PhSe2.TheReferenceFormBooksSteeringAsPower}).
  \item A fresh reference-form fit to the analytic plant's own flights still books 5.9\% of
        $\lvert y\T u\rvert$ through steering, and supplies energy by steering alone at 33 of 250
        held-out steps, although the plant it learned from books none
        (\code{PhSe2Train.BothFormsFitTheDynamicBicycle}). That fit is good: its held-out rollout
        loss falls from 8.52 to 0.0006. (These are the flights of the plant since \#852. On
        \#851's branch, before \#852's wheel-frame slip, they were 5.6\% and 74 of 250.)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Steering as a modulation.} libcvc \#851 adds an opt-in form that mirrors
Proposition~\ref{prop:ph}: \code{se2\_setup::modulation\_dim} (0 by default). With
$\texttt{modulation\_dim}=1$ the last input, the road-wheel angle $\delta$, stops being a port. It is
read by the GPs of $D$ and $G$ beside the twist:
\begin{equation}
  D(v,\delta)=\operatorname{spd}\big(\ell_D+\mathrm{GP}_D(v/s_v,\delta)\big),\qquad
  G(v,\delta)=\ell_G+\mathrm{GP}_G(v/s_v,\delta),\qquad y=G(v,\delta)\T v,
  \label{eq:learned-mod}
\end{equation}
with $u$ the drive alone in~\eqref{eq:learned}. The balance~\eqref{eq:learned-balance} holds
unchanged, so with the drive at zero nothing is supplied, whatever $\delta$ is. Steering still steers,
through $D$ and $G$, as the steered tire's secant does in~\eqref{eq:R}. The modulation is data, like
the inputs, so the reverse pass drops its gradient.
\begin{itemize}
  \item A modulated model is written as \code{cvc-ph-se2/2}; models without modulations stay
        \code{/1}, byte for byte.
  \item \code{cvc ph-train --steer-modulates} fits it from plant logs recorded with the actual
        road-wheel angle, which is \code{cvc-plant-log}'s default.
  \item The reference form stays the default and keeps its JAX parity. The modulated form has no
        JAX golden. It is held to central differences and to the analytic identities
        (Table~\ref{tab:val-steer}).
  \item On the analytic plant's flights, it learns as well (held-out loss $7.63\to0.0167$;
        $0.0092$ before \#852) with steering's share at zero. The reference form fits those flights more closely, so the
        modulation is a matter of correct books, not of accuracy.
  \item At Adam's default learning rate of $3\times10^{-3}$, the reference form's gradient norm blew
        up ($10^6$--$10^9$) in all three runs tried on that data, while the modulated form
        converged in all four configurations tried. That is one dataset (the flights before
        \#852) and one seed, so it is reported here, not claimed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The potential's form, and the wheels as inputs}
\label{sec:learned-potential}
The potential $V(x,y)$ of~\eqref{eq:learned} depends on world position, so a fit can tie energy to
where the vehicle drove rather than to how (Section~\ref{sec:review}). libcvc \#982 makes its form
an option, \code{se2\_setup::potential}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{world}: the $V(x,y)$ above, and the default;
  \item \code{none}: $H$ is the kinetic energy, and the body term is zero;
  \item \code{terrain}: the body term is two learned levels times the logged sines of the ground's
        slope along and across the heading, $(k_x\theta_\parallel,\,k_y\theta_\perp)$. That is the
        gradient of $mgz$ along the body's axes for a vehicle on that slope ($k\to mg$, up to $M$'s
        scale), read where the vehicle is rather than learned where it was, and its power is the
        climb's. $H$ cannot evaluate it from $(x,y)$, so \code{hamiltonian()} leaves it out.
\end{itemize}
A model whose potential is not \code{world} is written as \code{cvc-ph-se2/3}. The plant logs' wide
layout (\code{cvc plant-log --wide}) records the rigid-body simulator's wheel speeds, the pedals,
the drive's shaft and brake power, and the ground's slope under the wheels from their contacts.
\code{cvc ph-train --inputs} picks a model's inputs by column name, so the wheels' rim speeds can be
ports.

\#982 reports a comparison of these options: 192 flights of 5~s in each of two environments, a flat
swerve at a mean 12.9~m/s and a city scene with terrain and buildings at a mean 6.0~m/s, scored by
the mean model's rollout loss on clean 2~s windows, at seeds 0 and 1. These are training runs, not
tests, and they were not repeated for this paper. Trained on the city scene, the ground-slope term
had the best held-out loss, 0.472 and 0.441, against $V(x,y)$'s 0.499 and 0.467 and no potential's
0.503 and 0.465. The body's attitude, used as the slope instead, fits the suspension's pitch under
acceleration on flat ground and fails on slopes, which is why the logs take the ground's slope from
the wheels' contacts. The rim speeds as ports overfit the city scene (held-out 0.58--0.67). Between
the two environments, the difference in speed dominates every model.

\section{The navigator's energy identity and context work}
\label{sec:nav}

The plant's books say where the \emph{vehicle's} energy goes. The navigator has its own energy,
$H_{\mathrm{nav}}=\tfrac m2\lVert v\rVert^2+U_{\mathrm{nav}}$, on its point-mass surrogate
(\code{integrate\_surrogate\_material}). libcvc \#832 adds per-step books for that surrogate, and
books across ticks for the deployed drive's potential.

\subsection{The per-step identity}
One semi-implicit step of a point mass $m$ in a potential $U$ held fixed over the step
(coefficients frozen), with damping $\gamma$, a port force $F$ and a speed clamp, is
\begin{equation}
  v'=v+\tfrac hm(-g-\gamma v+F),\qquad \tilde v=\operatorname{clamp}_{v_{\max}}(v'),\qquad
  x'=x+h\tilde v,\qquad g=\nabla U(x).
  \label{eq:si-step}
\end{equation}
With $H=\tfrac m2\lVert v\rVert^2+U$, define the damping work
$W_\gamma=\tfrac h2\gamma\,v\cdot(v+v')$, the port work $W_{\mathrm{port}}=\tfrac h2F\cdot(v+v')$, the
clamp loss $W_{\mathrm{clamp}}=\tfrac m2(\lVert v'\rVert^2-\lVert\tilde v\rVert^2)\ge0$ and the
clamp cross term $W_\times=h\,g\cdot(\tilde v-v')$. Then the residual is
\begin{equation}
  r_E=\Delta H+W_\gamma+W_{\mathrm{clamp}}-W_{\mathrm{port}}-W_\times
  =\frac{h^2}{2}\Big[\tilde v\T\bar{\mathcal H}\,\tilde v-\frac1m\,g\cdot(g+\gamma v-F)\Big],
  \label{eq:rE}
\end{equation}
exactly, with $\bar{\mathcal H}=2\int_0^1(1-s)\nabla^2U(x+s\,\Delta x)\,ds$ the averaged Hessian.
(Expand $\tfrac m2(\lVert v'\rVert^2-\lVert v\rVert^2)=\tfrac h2(-g-\gamma v+F)\cdot(v+v')$, and
Taylor-expand $U(x')-U(x)=h\,g\cdot\tilde v+\tfrac{h^2}2\tilde v\T\bar{\mathcal H}\tilde v$.)
\code{book\_step} books any such step from its quantities. \code{rollout\_energy} carries the books
for every step of the material surrogate. They are evaluated in double, so they resolve terms
of order $h^3$ below float round-off on $U$.

\subsection{Two checks}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{The cheap bound}, every step:
        \begin{equation*}
          \lvert r_E\rvert\le\kappa_H\frac{h^2}2\Big[\frac{\lvert g\rvert\,(\lvert g\rvert+\gamma\lvert v\rvert+\lvert F\rvert)}m
          +\hat L\,\lvert\tilde v\rvert^2\Big],
        \end{equation*}
        with the secant Lipschitz estimate $\hat L=\lvert\nabla U(x')-\nabla U(x)\rvert/\lvert x'-x\rvert$
        and safety factor $\kappa_H=2$ (\code{energy\_residual\_bound}). It is a tolerance model,
        not a theorem, since $\hat L$ estimates the curvature and does not bound it. It catches
        work of order $h$ left out of the books: an unlogged force, a stale field, an unbooked
        clamp.
  \item \emph{The sharp check}, when asked (two extra force evaluations a step):
        $\hat r_E=\tfrac{h^2}2\big[\tilde v\T\nabla^2U(x)\tilde v-g\cdot(g+\gamma v-F)/m\big]$,
        with $r_E-\hat r_E=O(h^3)$. It catches a second-order error the identity does not predict. Explicit Euler
        ($x'=x+hv$) is the example: its $r_E$ sits $h^2g\cdot(g+\gamma v-F)/m$ above $\hat r_E$.
        That is second order, the same order as the bound, so explicit Euler passes the bound
        and fails the sharp check.
\end{itemize}
The explicit-Euler case mattered in practice. Through libcvc 3.7.11, \code{cvc::nav}'s native
multi-start rollout ran explicit Euler by default, where its Python namesake runs semi-implicit, as
GRL-SNAM's guide noted. On circular orbits of a goal spring,
\code{NavPotential.ExplicitEulerRaisesTheAlarm} shows explicit Euler gaining energy at every step,
by $\omega^2h^2$, with the sharp check at half the bound on every step. The semi-implicit step
meets the sharp check to $3.5\times10^{-5}$ of the bound.

\paragraph{The integrator.} libcvc's geometry rollout (\code{integrate\_surrogate\_v2}, which the
material trainer's multi-start loss uses) now defaults to semi-implicit Euler~\eqref{eq:si-step},
as every other point-mass rollout already did: the velocity updates first, and the position steps
with the new velocity (transfix/libcvc \#981). Explicit Euler remains an option, and the sharp
check still alarms on it. On \code{nav\_geom\_rollout\_grad\_test}'s penetrating batch the
multi-start penalty is 8\% lower under semi-implicit Euler on the legacy start (2.050 against
2.230) and 15\% lower on sampled starts (2.453 against 2.898).

\#981 also reports an A/B, a training run outside CI that was not repeated for this paper. It
trained the coefficient policy on the point mass under each integrator, ten training seeds per arm
on the city GRL-SNAM trains on. It then drove every policy on the rigid-body simulator's 1/10-scale
vehicles, over 20 seeds of 48 agents, with paired bootstrap 95\% intervals.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Where training moves the policy (learning rates $10^{-4}$ and $2\times10^{-4}$), explicit
        Euler costs reach: $-7.3$ $[-10.1,-4.7]$ and $-4.7$ $[-8.4,-1.6]$ points.
  \item At the gate's learning rate, $5\times10^{-5}$, the arms barely differ: explicit Euler gains
        $+1.0$ $[+0.1,+1.9]$ points of reach, but drives 5\% further and hits buildings 4\% more.
  \item On the trained policies' point-mass steps, booked outside every barrier, the sharp check
        never alarms under semi-implicit Euler (its largest ratio is $1.2\times10^{-3}$). Under
        explicit Euler it alarms on 14--19\% of steps, and 78--94\% of those gained energy.
\end{itemize}
The alarm fires on a minority of steps because the trained point mass is heavily damped and near
its terminal velocity, where $g+\gamma v-F\approx0$ and explicit Euler's excess
$h^2g\cdot(g+\gamma v-F)/m$ vanishes. A Strang splitting, velocity Verlet with the damping and the
speed clamp split out, never differed from semi-implicit Euler outside the interval and was not
kept. GRL-SNAM's native material trainer leaves the integrator to libcvc on its legacy start, so
with a \code{pycvc} that carries \#981 that start runs semi-implicit, while its torch twin keeps
explicit Euler as its default.

\subsection{Context work and projection work}
Across a tick of the deployed drive, $U_{\mathrm{nav}}$ is not fixed. The occupancy map is re-sensed,
the field rebuilt, the coefficients re-predicted, and the carrot moved. Those changes do work on
the navigator's energy that no step's books see. \code{sim\_world} books them in its drive
telemetry while it captures:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{potential\_end}: $U$ at the pose the tick ends at, against the tick's field;
  \item \code{projection\_work}: $U$'s change across anything that moves the agent after the drive,
        the kinematic override and the \code{min\_gap} de-overlap: \code{potential\_end} minus $U$
        where the drive (or the plant) left the agent;
  \item \code{context\_work}: $U$ at the tick's start pose with this tick's context, minus $U$ at
        the same pose with last tick's context (coefficients, carrot, field, material planes),
        evaluated as \code{step()} begins.
\end{itemize}
When nothing moves the agent between ticks, \code{context\_work} equals this tick's starting $U$
minus last tick's \code{potential\_end}, bit for bit. The potential's change over a run then
telescopes:
\begin{equation}
  U_K^{\mathrm{end}}-U_1^{\mathrm{start}}=\sum_{k=1}^K\big(U_k^{\mathrm{drive}}-U_k^{\mathrm{start}}\big)
  +\sum_{k=1}^K\text{projection}_k+\sum_{k=2}^K\text{context}_k ,
  \label{eq:telescope}
\end{equation}
where $U_k^{\mathrm{drive}}$ is $U$ where the drive left the agent on tick $k$. The first sum is
what the drive's motion did under a fixed context. The other two are what the runtime did to the
landscape. The drive itself leaves the three fields zero. The bicycle and dynamic rollouts are not
point masses, so they reject requests for per-step point-mass books (they throw). For a vehicle,
the plant's books of Section~\ref{sec:books} are the counterpart.

\subsection{Two sets of books, not one}
\label{sec:two-books}
When a navigation world drives the dynamic plant, there are two sets of books. The plant books
the vehicle's kinetic energy~\eqref{eq:live-books}. The telemetry books the navigator's
potential~\eqref{eq:telescope}. They are not one conserved quantity. $U_{\mathrm{nav}}$ is a
learned landscape whose coefficients change every tick, not a physical energy of the vehicle. The
two meet only through the controller, which is not a power-conserving interconnection. Keeping
both, with their own residuals, is what makes each one checkable.

\subsection{The twins and their parity tiers}

from its own test and GRL-SNAM copies it into \code{tests/fixtures}. The twin is then held to the
golden at a stated tier:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{double}: both sides in float64 from the same parameters, to $10^{-9}$ relative;
  \item \emph{float}: libcvc in float32 against the twin in float32, to about $10^{-5}$;
  \item \emph{mixed}: libcvc's float rollouts against the twin in float64, to $10^{-4}$ on values and
        $10^{-3}$ on gradients.

\subsection{Using the plant and its books from GRL-SNAM}
What follows is what GRL-SNAM can do with what is merged today, and the small pieces it would add.
Pieces not yet built are marked as proposals.

\paragraph{Train through the dynamic plant.} The dynamic rollouts are twinned and differentiable:
\code{integrate\_dynamic\_material} for the material bicycle, and \code{dyn4\_rollout\_ext} for the
four-wheel vehicle with an external force. A trainer that today rolls out the kinematic bicycle can
roll out \code{dyn\_bike.dyn\_plant} in its place, under the same \code{bike\_control} commands, and
backpropagate through tires, load transfer and actuator lag. In C++ the same choice is
\code{coef\_train}'s \code{rollout\_kind::dynamic}. In a live world it is
\code{plant\_host.use(sim, "dynamic")}.

\paragraph{Book the energy.} The torch twin of the books (\#138) is small, because
\code{dyn\_bike.py}'s step already computes every part: the axle forces $F_{x,w}$, the lateral forces
$F_{y,w}$, the secants $S_w$ and the forward speeds $u_w$. The rates at a state
(\code{dyn\_ph\_rates\_at}) are
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.86\linewidth}
\small
\begin{verbatim}
nf = (-sin d, cos d, lf cos d);  nr = (0, 1, -lr)
tf = (cos d, sin d, lf sin d);   tr = (1, 0, 0)
R  = cx e_x e_x^T + Sf nf nf^T + Sr nr nr^T
supplied   = v . (tf Fxf + tr Fxr)          # y^T u
dissipated = v^T R v                        # >= 0
floor      = v . (nf (Fyf + Sf nf.v) + nr (Fyr + Sr nr.v))   # round-off
grade      = -m g theta vx
\end{verbatim}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}
The books of a step (\code{dyn\_plant\_books}) are the trapezoid of these between the start (with
the step's actuator outputs) and the pre-limiter twist, plus $H$'s change across the limiters
(Section~\ref{sec:books}). \code{tests/test\_dyn\_bike\_ph\_parity.py} holds them to libcvc's
\code{DynBikePH.Golden} in double: the worst relative difference is $7.1\times10^{-14}$ on the
books and $4.4\times10^{-14}$ on their gradient, which autograd takes and libcvc takes by dual
numbers.

\paragraph{Book the navigator's energy.} The point-mass surrogate's books come with its rollout.
Given \code{energy=\{"books": True, "sharp": True\}}, \code{integrate\_surrogate\_material} fills
\code{kinetic}, \code{w\_damp}, \code{w\_port}, \code{w\_clamp}, \code{w\_cross}, \code{residual},
\code{bound} and \code{residual\_sharp} for every step, in float64 and detached, as libcvc's
\code{rollout\_energy} does. The rollout's outputs are unchanged bit for bit.
\code{nav\_potential.book\_step} books any other such step; the parity test books
\code{sdf\_rollout}'s clamped surrogate with it. \code{tests/test\_nav\_energy\_books\_parity.py}
holds the books to \code{NavPotential.BooksGolden}. \code{book\_step} on libcvc's arguments agrees
bit for bit, and the books agree to $1.1\times10^{-6}$ of the step's bound on the clamped rollout
and $8.3\times10^{-8}$ on the material surrogate. The books see only the integrator when the
patch's gradient channels are its values' gradients. Otherwise each step also books the gap
between the force and $-\nabla U$, work of order $h$, which the cheap bound reads as a stale field:
on \code{NavPotential.Golden}'s curved patch it fills 99.65\% of the bound at $h=0.002$. Across
ticks, a live world's books come from \code{pycvc} (libcvc \#857). With capture on,
\code{nav\_sim\_world\_drive\_telemetry} gives each agent's \code{projection\_work} and
\code{context\_work}, the terms of the last two sums of~\eqref{eq:telescope}.

\paragraph{Energy-aware objectives (proposal).} With the books available inside the rollout, a
trainer can price energy directly:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\sum\max(y\T u,0)\,h$, the drive energy, as a cost;
  \item the dissipated energy $\sum v\T\R v\,h$ as a proxy for tire scrub and sliding (it grows
        sharply past the peak);
  \item the limiter energy as a penalty for leaning on the speed clamp.
\end{itemize}
Each is differentiable almost everywhere, as the plant is. Because steering does no work, the
drive energy counts every joule the policy puts into the vehicle.

\paragraph{Health checks.} The books double as run-time checks of a rollout:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the residual's share of the flow (in the tests' runs, $1.1\times10^{-4}$ to
        $1.2\times10^{-3}$ at $h=0.01$~s) flags a step too long for the motion;
  \item a floor line above round-off flags a plant whose slips no longer root at $n_w\T v=0$;
  \item a limiter line that dominates flags a policy that lives at the speed clamp.
\end{itemize}
On the navigator side, the cheap bound flags an unlogged force or an unbooked clamp, and the sharp
check flags an explicit-Euler integrator.

\paragraph{New force terms are ports.} GRL-SNAM's research extensions add force terms to the
navigator: material and terrain costs, and external forces. Section~\ref{sec:ports} gives the rule.
A conservative term goes in $U_{\mathrm{nav}}$ with its force $-\nabla U$, which the twin's
autograd gradcheck verifies. A non-conservative term is a port, booked as $W_{\mathrm{port}}$ on
the surrogate. On the vehicle, it is either already in $y\T u$ (if it acts through the controller)
or a new column $\G_e$ (if it acts on the body).

\section{Limitations and future work}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The four-wheel vehicle's form.} Three choices in Section~\ref{sec:four-wheel} are left
as the plant makes them, since changing any of them changes the plant, its goldens and GRL-SNAM's
\code{dyn4.py} twin:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The lever is one-way. Making it reciprocal means adding the roll and pitch rates to each
        contact patch's lateral velocity.
  \item The grade force is off by default, so a vehicle climbs a hill for free unless its host
        turns it on.
  \item The dampers act on the body's own vertical rates, not on its rates relative to the ground,
        so a steady climb pays them too. The books show that as dissipation.
\end{itemize}
Without wheel spin, the four-wheel form and its books have no torch twin yet, and the twin does not
have the grade force. The four-wheel plant has no compliant contact.

\paragraph{Structure-preserving integrators.} Neither plant's default integrator keeps a discrete
energy identity. Both are first order (analytic) or second order (learned) with a booked residual.
The single track's opt-in discrete-gradient steps (Section~\ref{sec:dg}) close their books
exactly. Without wheel spin the step costs about ten times the default's and is not L-stable; with
it, about twenty times, and its wheels' rows at the step's end cost their speeds an order of
accuracy and book the scheme's own damping. \code{pycvc} binds neither, and GRL-SNAM has no twin
of either. The four-wheel and learned plants have none.

\paragraph{The learned plant's evidence bound.} The rollout objective does not fit the diffusion or
an observation model. LieSPHGP's EKF-based ELBO is the next stage for \code{cvc::ph}. Beyond the
synthetic comparison of Section~\ref{sec:learned-steer}, the fit's accuracy against the analytic
plant is not reported here.

\paragraph{The learned plant's steering, upstream.} The modulated form of
Section~\ref{sec:learned-steer} exists only in libcvc. It has no JAX golden, and whether
LieSPHGP or the $SE(2)$ package adopts it is for their authors to decide.

\paragraph{The closed loop.} The navigator commands the vehicle through a path-following
controller, not an energy-shaping one, so the closed loop is not port-Hamiltonian, and the two
sets of books of Section~\ref{sec:two-books} stay separate. Interconnection and damping assignment for
a car is a research problem: with two inputs and three velocity degrees of freedom, its matching
condition fails in general~\cite{ortega2002idapbc}.

\paragraph{Python access.} The plant step's books and the navigator's point-mass books have torch
twins (GRL-SNAM \#138 and \#139), and \code{pycvc} reads a running world's tick telemetry
(libcvc \#857). \code{pycvc.dyn\_bike\_plant} (libcvc \#979, \code{781b9a2f}, released in 3.7.9)
puts a navigation world on the live plant, optionally in a rigid-body world
the caller builds, and reads its books per tick and since attach. GRL-SNAM's \code{capture} shows
them while it drives (GRL-SNAM \#143). libcvc \#1002 (\code{1ded7bed}, released in 3.7.9) binds
the options of Section~\ref{sec:ext} and the slip and compliant-contact lines of the
books, with a ground height from a \code{pycvc.height\_field} and a contact field copied from a
\code{sim\_world}. libcvc \#1038 (\code{2434c33a}, released in 3.7.12) binds \code{contact\_step\_depth}
(\#1004). Its test runs Section~\ref{sec:contact}'s wall impact at the default 0.01~s substep,
where \#1002's ran it at 0.005~s: the contact dissipates $8.16\times10^{4}$~J, the speed clamp
takes nothing, and the books leave $1.3\times10^{-3}$ of the flow.
\code{pycvc} does not bind \code{dyn\_4w\_plant} or \code{plant\_host}.

\subsection{Gaps raised in review, and the work on them}
\label{sec:review}
Review of this paper raised three gaps between the models it describes and the motion they are
meant to capture. libcvc has since closed all three: the first two with opt-in extensions
(Section~\ref{sec:ext}), and the third with a semi-implicit default for the navigator
(Section~\ref{sec:nav}) and an opt-in discrete-gradient step for the plant (Section~\ref{sec:dg}).
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Actuation.} Neither analytic plant had wheel spin: the drive and brake forces were
        set within the tires' grip, with no wheel speed or longitudinal slip in the state. The
        learned plant's training rows had no wheel speeds either, and its potential $V(x,y)$
        depends on world position, so a fit can tie energy to where the vehicle drove rather than
        to how. libcvc \#982 adds wheel-speed states with a (wheel torque, wheel speed) port and
        the longitudinal slip's secants in $\R$, in both analytic plants and the live plant
        (Section~\ref{sec:spin}); the rigid-body simulator's wheel speeds in its logs; and the
        option to drop $V$ or replace it by the logged ground slope
        (Section~\ref{sec:learned-potential}). The defaults are unchanged.
  \item \emph{Collisions.} Neither pH form had contact. The live plants collide in a rigid-body
        world (Section~\ref{sec:live}), but by handing the model the world's state, with the
        energy that changes booked as contact, outside the form, and \code{cut\_flights} dropped
        every window with a contact, an impact or a wheel off the ground. libcvc \#983 adds
        compliant contact to the single track's form, with a barrier energy in $H$ and the
        contact's damping and friction in $\R$, entering as an external port, against the scene
        and between vehicles (Section~\ref{sec:contact}); and the logs keep the contact windows on
        request. libcvc \#1004 makes the contact's damper and friction implicit, so fast impacts
        close their books at the default substep. The four-wheel plant has no compliant contact,
        a learned plant can fit a contact's mean response but not where it is, and inside a thick
        wall the navigation world's field is flat (Section~\ref{sec:contact}).
  \item \emph{Integration.} libcvc's native multi-start rollout of the navigator defaulted to
        explicit Euler (\code{geom\_rollout\_params::semi\_implicit} was false), which gains energy
        at every step on the orbits of Section~\ref{sec:nav}. libcvc \#981 makes semi-implicit
        Euler the default, with an A/B comparison (Section~\ref{sec:nav}). libcvc \#978 adds an
        opt-in discrete-gradient step for the single-track plant, whose books close to the solve's
        tolerance (Section~\ref{sec:dg}), and libcvc \#1045 extends it to the wheel-spin plant.
        The defaults' books still keep a residual, and neither discrete-gradient step has Python
        access.
\end{enumerate}
\paragraph{Recordings.} A reel of GRL-SNAM's navigator on vehicle plants, with three successes and
five failures, is published as a GRL-SNAM pre-release:
\url{https://github.com/CVC-Lab/GRL-SNAM/releases/tag/plant-reel-2026-11}. Each clip is one
\code{grl-snam capture} run of GRL-SNAM's shipped coefficient policy (\code{coef\_sdf.pt}) on a
city scene whose buildings are OpenStreetMap footprints (\textcopyright{} OpenStreetMap
contributors, ODbL). Its read-out shows the sideslip, the share of the tire's peak grip in use,
the lateral acceleration and the energy books. Each clip but the first has a JSON file with its
measured outcome. The plants are the policy's point-mass surrogate, the navigator's kinematic
bicycle, the live plant of Section~\ref{sec:live} and the rigid-body simulator's vehicle, both of
these last two with the full-size preset.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Successes.} The surrogate reaches four goals, retargeted live, each within its
        trained radius of 240~m. The kinematic bicycle drives 296~m up a street and arrives within
        15~m in 5.6~s; having no tires, it runs at the trained metric's speeds, up to 115~m/s.
        The rigid-body vehicle, on grip 0.3, drives 234~m around a block's corner and arrives
        within 15~m.
  \item \emph{Failures.} The kinematic bicycle stalls at a dead end 700~m short: the policy's
        wall-following escape needs the point mass's sideways step. The rigid-body vehicle, on
        grip 0.9, circles at 7~m/s, because the wall-following target sits inside its turning
        circle. On grip 0.3 the navigator asks the live plant to corner at 0.5~g when its tires
        hold 0.3~g; it slides past the tires' peak, to $67^\circ$ of slip, and pins itself against
        a wall for 530 ticks. The rigid-body vehicle spins out on grip 0.3, up to $90^\circ$ off
        its path. With the map unknown, on grip 0.2 at up to 25~m/s, it hits a wall it cannot
        stop for.
\end{itemize}
What fails is the policy and the navigator's plan, not the plants. The policy was trained on the
surrogate, where arrival is within 240~m, and does not transfer to a vehicle that must arrive
within 15~m; the navigator caps cornering at 0.5~g whatever the grip. Over 24 random routes of
120--400~m in the same scene, it arrives on 3 with the surrogate, 3 with the kinematic bicycle,
and none with the live plant or the rigid-body vehicle on grip 0.9 (GRL-SNAM's
\code{docs/PLANTS.md}). The clips were rendered with GRL-SNAM \#143 and libcvc \#979.
